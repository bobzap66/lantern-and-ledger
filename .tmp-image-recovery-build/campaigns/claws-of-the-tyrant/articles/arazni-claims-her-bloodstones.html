Arazni Claims Her Bloodstones

Arazni Claims Her Bloodstones

Properties1
session recordsThe Goddess Gets a Vote

Sep 30, 202613 min read

The Lantern and Ledger

A conclave convened to decide how the Knights of Lastwall should use two recovered Bloodstones of Arazni ended with neither relic in mortal hands. Six investigators left the chamber during a recess, returned saying they had received Arazni’s answer directly, and reported that the goddess wanted every remaining Bloodstone recovered and returned to her for destruction.

By Tavian Rusk, War Correspondent
Events: Gozran 17, 4725 AR

Contributors

Tavian Rusk War Correspondent

VELLUMIS — The Knights of Lastwall began their conclave with two of Arazni’s Bloodstones, several competing proposals for what to do with them and no agreement that the goddess herself had any practical role in the decision. By the time the meeting adjourned, both relics had been surrendered to Arazni, the Knights had committed themselves to recovering the remaining two on her behalf, and one of the six investigators from Fort Corlach had sworn himself to the faction of knights whose oath is made directly to her.

That sequence is easier to summarize than it was to reach. The formal purpose of the meeting was to hear what had happened at Fort Corlach and Yua’s Hope, establish how two of the four Bloodstones had come into the Knights’ possession and determine what should happen before the undead searching for the same relics found the others. Aylunna Varvatos presided. The room included senior Knights of Lastwall, representatives of several factions and institutions, the six investigators recently returned from Corlach, and the survivors of Yua’s Hope who had carried another Bloodstone through six years of occupation and flight.

The first report concerned Corlach. Sebastian Cuthbert Farrington IV gave the broad outline of the investigation before Jwayne Dawnson and Goldie Dawnstar supplied details with less concern for preserving the dignity of the proceedings. They described the systematic undead searches of former Ozem positions, the ancient guardians beneath Corlach, the Bloodstone hidden in the vault and the creature that had impersonated Addit Kalakin before escaping. Ray Lightbrand then filled the chamber with healing energy at Farrington’s request. No concealed undead reacted. Given what had happened beneath Corlach, this was one of the few interruptions to the agenda that nobody present appears to have considered excessive.

Jwayne Dawnson, Ray Lightbrand, Goldie Dawnstar, Sebastian Cuthbert Farrington IV, Bram, Wilfred, Throgg, and Tess take part in the Castle Varitan conclave over Arazni’s Bloodstones. Artist’s reconstruction based on participant accounts.

Dawnson eventually placed the Spleen Bloodstone on the table. Witnesses agree that recognition spread through the room immediately. The investigators explained that four alcoves had existed in the protected chamber beneath Corlach, although only one still contained a Bloodstone. Varvatos and Kalabrynne Iomedar drew the same conclusion from the fort’s construction: Corlach had probably been intended to protect all four relics. By the time the six reached it, three were already gone.

The second report supplied the whereabouts of one of them. The survivors of Yua’s Hope described the years spent beneath the ruined settlement, the death of Dame Talia Gildar, the corruption that forced them out of the crypt where they had survived and their flight toward Vellumis while undead forces moved through the region behind them. Tomas Vale then explained how Constan Russi had entrusted him with a key and instructions to open a locked box if Russi died. After Russi’s death, Vale found the Lung Bloodstone inside.

The relic later made the survivors a target. Omelia, a known agent of the Whispering Way, pursued them during their escape. Vale transferred the Bloodstone to Belren before making a stand intended to buy him enough time to flee. Belren did not flee. Neither did the others. Omelia was killed, and every surviving member of the group reached Vellumis with the Bloodstone still in their possession. The distinction matters because much of the discussion that followed concerned what qualified someone to make decisions about these objects. The people at the lower end of the table had fewer titles than most of the room. They also had six years of practical experience keeping one of the relics away from the dead.

With half of the known set assembled, the meeting shifted from reports to proposals. Some argued that the four Bloodstones together might give the Knights a weapon capable of reclaiming Lastwall. Others suggested that the relics could be distributed among trustworthy knights and used separately for their individual powers without attempting to compel Arazni herself. A third proposal was to place them in secure custody elsewhere, while at least one attendee suggested transferring them to an institution willing to compensate the Knights for doing so.

Goldie Dawnstar objected to the premise beneath most of those proposals. If the Bloodstones were parts of Arazni, she argued, then possession did not make them the Knights’ property to use. Lieutenant Gauvry asked why the Knights should not gather all four and control Arazni if doing so could help retake Lastwall. Dawnstar’s answer was uncomplicated: “Because she’s a goddess and controlling people is bad.”

Dawnson reached a similar conclusion by a different route. He initially argued that Arazni’s own doctrine of survival might permit the use of the Bloodstones’ lesser powers. “I believe she, in her heart, would be an organ donor,” he told the conclave. Farrington questioned whether that reasoning simply returned them to deciding what Arazni ought to want rather than asking her.

The argument might have continued considerably longer had Beirivelle Starshine not announced that there was a means of obtaining a clearer answer. She requested a recess and asked Dawnson, Dawnstar, Lightbrand, Farrington, Ren Swifttail and Serafina to accompany her.

What occurred during that recess cannot be fully reported here. All six participants later described themselves as having learned information they had agreed not to disclose, and I have no intention of converting their confidence into a map for anyone who might prefer that knowledge in hostile hands. The relevant result can be established without doing so: the six say they communicated directly with Arazni, and Clarethe Iomedar, leader of the Crimson Reclaimers, later entered the conclave and publicly vouched for their account.

The interviews do establish one historical revelation that the participants considered necessary to understanding Arazni’s answer. According to the accounts given to me, they learned that the commonly repeated history of Arazni’s service during the Shining Crusade leaves out a consequential fact. The Knights of Ozem did not merely summon Arazni and ask her to fight Tar-Baphon. Fearing that she might refuse or be compromised, they bound her to their will.

Dawnson asked whether this meant Arazni had been compelled to fight rather than volunteering. He was told that she had. Dawnstar’s response during my interview was essentially the same response others remember from the recess: “They lied?”

There is an important distinction between this act and the later creation of the Bloodstones. The relics did not exist when Arazni first fought Tar-Baphon. After the Whispering Tyrant killed her, agents of Geb stole her body and carried it south. Her body was mutilated during the ritual that raised her as a lich, and the preserved pieces became the Bloodstones now being recovered. Later accounts conflate the separate abuses easily enough. The people involved in them had the advantage of several centuries in which to make the story less complicated.

The six would not describe the means by which they reached Arazni, and I did not ask them to break the promise that prevented them from doing so. They did agree on why they sought her answer. Dawnson said he wanted Arazni to decide whether the Bloodstones should be used rather than having mortals infer her wishes. Lightbrand agreed. Serafina said much the same in the language of fate. Farrington observed that Arazni’s death had already resulted from someone else’s choice and suggested that she might now be allowed to speak for herself.

Dawnstar put the matter most plainly. “It’s not right to make somebody do something they don’t want to do.”

Their accounts of Arazni’s answer are notably consistent. I cannot independently verify the circumstances of the communication, but I can report that six separate participants describe the same central exchange, that Iomedar later vouched publicly for their account and that the Bloodstones brought into the recess did not return with them.

Arazni receives Jwayne Dawnson, Ray Lightbrand, Goldie Dawnstar, Sebastian Cuthbert Farrington IV, Serafina, and Ren Swifttail and claims the recovered Bloodstones. Artist’s reconstruction based on participant accounts; the means and location of the audience are intentionally omitted.

Arazni’s position, as related to me by every participant willing to discuss it, left little room for interpretation. She wanted the Bloodstones returned. If return proved impossible, she wanted them destroyed. Their existence, she said, continued the violation that created them and left open the possibility that others might again use them to bind her.

“Anyone who thinks of keeping them is my enemy, regardless of intentions,” Dawnson recalled her saying.

He surrendered the Spleen Bloodstone immediately. Serafina handed over the Lung Bloodstone she had been carrying on behalf of the group. Dawnstar later told me that she asked whether Arazni intended to keep them. She did not receive a direct answer, although none of those present saw either relic again.

The discussion also supplied a name behind the present danger. Arazni identified Seldeg Bhedlis, a former Knight of Ozem who later became a grave knight, as a continuing threat. Bhedlis had traveled to Geb with other knights, embraced undeath there and served as one of Arazni’s jailers after her transformation. He now operates from Hammerrock and possesses the Chalice of Ozem, an artifact taken during a recent attack on Fort Ozem. Arazni warned that the symbolic importance of the chalice might allow Bhedlis to continue whatever ritual he is pursuing even without the Bloodstones.

She could not tell the six where the remaining Bloodstones were hidden. The surviving relics remain in mortal hands and, by her explanation, beyond the direct reach of divine sight. She suggested that the most likely locations would be old vaults or safe houses established by the Crimson Keepers, who once scattered the stones to prevent anyone from possessing the entire set.

The six returned from the recess with more than instructions. They described receiving Arazni’s favor and a weapon formed from their mingled blood and hers, known as Arazni’s Freedom. The details of its enchantments matter less here than the fact that the group understood the gift as both aid and commission. When they returned to the conclave with Clarethe Iomedar, none of them treated the question of whether the Bloodstones should be used as still open.

Dawnstar began by telling the assembled Knights that they should tell the truth and apologize to Arazni. Dawnson, perhaps correctly judging that the meeting might otherwise acquire a second agenda, summarized the operational decision instead: Arazni did not want anyone using her Bloodstones. She wanted the remaining two found and returned for destruction.

Iomedar publicly vouched for the account. Starshine asked whether anyone objected. Several witnesses remember people in the room appearing dissatisfied, including Varvatos, but no one formally challenged the decision. The conclave ended with the Knights committed to supporting the six investigators in recovering the remaining relics according to Arazni’s wishes.

That decision did not settle every question raised during the recess. Dawnstar emerged troubled by the history she had learned. Her service as a Knight Vigilant had rested in part on stories about the institutions she trusted, and at least one of those stories had proved carefully incomplete. Afterward, Dawnson told her that the truth could not be spread while the war against Tar-Baphon remained unresolved. She agreed that secrecy might presently be necessary. Agreement and acceptance are not the same thing.

Iomedar later provided the group with arms and supplies from the Crimson Reclaimers’ stores. She also offered them the Crimson Oath. Dawnson accepted. The decision places a Pathfinder who began this mission as an outside specialist formally within one of the Knights of Lastwall’s two principal factions, and specifically within the faction whose allegiance is closely bound to Arazni.

The company used the remaining time in Vellumis to prepare. Dawnson researched the creature that escaped Corlach and identified it as a skulltaker, an undead horror capable of tearing bones from nearby corpses and raising the dead as skeletal warriors. This answers one question left by my previous report. It does not answer where the skulltaker went, whether Addit Kalakin still lives or what preparations the creature has made after escaping with knowledge of Lightbrand’s methods.

The final intelligence came from Starshine. Knights scouts had observed suspicious concentrations of undead around two locations. The first is Dortrot Ranch, whose ordinary function gives no obvious reason for a major undead movement. The second is Castle Firrine, an abandoned stronghold from the early war against Tar-Baphon. Scouts report that undead forces have approached the castle but have been unable to enter its grounds.

Beirivelle Starshine briefs Jwayne Dawnson, Ray Lightbrand, Goldie Dawnstar, Sebastian Cuthbert Farrington IV, Serafina, and Ren Swifttail on undead movements near Dortrot Ranch and Castle Firrine, with Hammerrock between the two leads. Artist’s reconstruction based on participant accounts.

Those movements may indicate the locations of the remaining Bloodstones, or merely the next links in the chain leading to them. Starshine could not establish more from the reports available. What can be said is that the search has changed. Eight days earlier, six investigators entered Corlach without knowing what the undead wanted. They now know what is being hunted, who has claimed ownership of it and at least one of the men who may be trying to seize it.

They also know that the owner wants her property back.
