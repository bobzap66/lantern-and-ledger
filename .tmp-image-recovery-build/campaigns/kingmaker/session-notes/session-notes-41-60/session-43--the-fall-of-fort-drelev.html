Session 43: The Fall of Fort Drelev

Session 43: The Fall of Fort Drelev

Properties1

From Linzi’s Journal

An account of events in late Calistril, 4714 AR, reconstructed from the testimony of those present

Hannis Drelev is dead.

It seems appropriate that the day which ended his rule began with feeding the people he had failed.

The Tubthumpers had already seen what years beneath Drelev had done to Fort Drelev. Shops stood empty, soldiers took what they wanted, and families struggled beneath taxes imposed by a baron who somehow always found money for his own comforts. Before turning their attention to the keep, Velka taught Ally the magic needed to create food, and the company distributed it among the town’s hungry children.

Then they went looking for the secret passage beneath the castle.

Satinder Morne had told them of an escape tunnel leading beneath the walls and into the caverns below Drelev Keep. The passage proved considerably less convenient than its original builders presumably intended. A flooded cavern concealed two enormous oozes, and when the creatures were attacked, they began splitting apart. Two became three, and the battle threatened to produce an increasingly unreasonable number of oozes before the Tubthumpers managed to destroy them.

The caverns held more than monsters. Hidden caches contained a remarkable collection of valuables, including enchanted armor and weapons, potions, poisons, gemstones, old tribal artifacts, and a greater ring of acid resistance resembling a ring once carried by Mairi. Most memorable was a magical knapsack made for halflings, containing an extradimensional compartment, a seemingly endless supply of cookware, and four healing berry tarts that replenish themselves each morning.

Having survived the tunnel and thoroughly investigated everything worth stealing from it, the Tubthumpers reached the dungeons beneath Drelev Keep.

There they found Terrion Numesti.

The imprisoned knight was sick and weakened but very much alive. The company picked the locks securing him, only for a badly timed cough to alert two guards nearby. One attempted to investigate while the other remained behind. The resulting fight was brief. Tarrion armed himself from the dead and, once the immediate danger had passed, gave the Tubthumpers a detailed description of the keep.

The ground floor contained kitchens, workshops, storage rooms, and access to the courtyard where Drelev’s giants and soldiers were quartered. Aemon Trask kept rooms on the second floor. Imeckus Stroon occupied the third. Hannis Drelev, his wife Pavetta Stroon-Drelev, and Drelev’s “guest,” Lady Quintessa Maray, occupied the fourth.

Tarrion departed to hide and begin making plans to recover his daughter Tamary Numesti. The Tubthumpers continued upward, although not before relieving Drelev of valuables from his treasury, a concealed vault, and his wine cellar. Liberation is an expensive undertaking.

Magic rendered the company invisible as they entered the occupied portions of the keep, but invisibility does little to prevent people from making noise. Guards in the ground-floor foyer realized something was wrong. Matteo attacked, breaking the company’s concealment, and one of the guards immediately ran for reinforcements.

Seamus chased him.

The pursuit climbed one flight of stairs, then another, then another. Seamus finally caught and killed the man on the fourth floor just as the guard threw open a door.

On the other side stood Lady Pavetta Stroon-Drelev.

Pavetta was understandably surprised to discover that the revolution had arrived in her bedroom. Her small dog, Jewel, was considerably louder about it.

What followed was less a battle than a negotiation conducted under exceptionally poor circumstances. Pavetta had little affection for her husband’s rule and considerably more interest in preserving her own position. The possibility emerged of removing Hannis while allowing Pavetta and her brother Imeckus to retain control of Fort Drelev under some arrangement with Thumping Waters.

The Tubthumpers discussed the proposal.

Velka eventually reduced the issue to its essential point. Fort Drelev could not truly be liberated while the people who had prospered from its oppression simply inherited what Hannis left behind.

“I do not believe that it will be possible for the people of Fort Drelev to be free and you to sustain the life in which you live.”

Pavetta went to consult her brother.

Imeckus Stroon proved more practical than either his sister or his brother-in-law. He was arrogant, condescending, and plainly concerned first with the interests of his own family, but he had no intention of dying for Hannis Drelev. He proposed an arrangement: the Tubthumpers would eliminate Hannis and his mistress, while Pavetta remained in authority under negotiated restrictions. In exchange, Imeckus and his sister would cooperate with Thumping Waters.

No final agreement was reached, but Imeckus provided something immediately useful. Hannis was likely dining in the banquet hall with Aemon Trask and several trusted guards.

The Tubthumpers went downstairs.

Hannis Drelev had survived for years by choosing submission whenever courage became expensive. He had surrendered hostages to the Tiger Lords, allied himself with Pitax, imprisoned those who opposed him, and sent an army into Thumping Waters while remaining safely behind his own walls.

Those walls no longer protected him.

The Tubthumpers stormed the banquet hall. Hannis, Aemon Trask, Lady Quintessa, and four guards were waiting inside. Whatever political negotiations remained for Fort Drelev would have to wait until the people responsible for its present misery were dealt with.

The fighting ended with Hannis Drelev dead.

Aemon Trask, who had escaped the battlefield at Tatzlford, did not escape a second time. He died beside the man whose army he had commanded. Three of the four guards were also killed.

Lady Quintessa proved more difficult to hold accountable. Rather than remain to share her lover’s fate, she escaped by teleportation.

When the fighting stopped, the ruler of Fort Drelev lay dead in his own banquet hall.

That did not make Fort Drelev free.

Pavetta and Imeckus remained. The Tiger Lords still held Tamary Numesti and other hostages. Soldiers and giants remained within the town, and no one had yet decided what government would replace the one the Tubthumpers had just destroyed. Killing a tyrant is a much simpler task than building something better in the space he leaves behind.

But for the first time since Hannis Drelev raised his walls beside Hooktongue Slough, that question belonged to someone other than Hannis Drelev.

The day began with hungry children.

It ended without a baron.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication
