Session 60: The Years Between

Session 60: The Years Between

Republic Chronicle — Restricted

Compiled by Linzi, Minister of Communication. Not for public dissemination.

Mairi’s Rite at Sevenarches

The spirit within Amiri’s greatsword could not be released by an ordinary exorcism. The druids of Sevenarches determined that the curse required a willing death inflicted by the blade. Mairi consented to be killed with the sword and was subsequently resurrected.

Outcome: Mairi returned alive. The spirit was freed. The malevolent ghost pursuing the trapped spirit no longer has the same reason to hunt the sword.

Mairi was unable to locate Amiri, the surviving Six Bears, or the Tiger Lords after the rite. Their current status and location remain unknown. The cleansed sword is presently displayed above the mantle in Mairi’s home.

Candlemere Spiral Seal

Research using the Republic’s archives and documents recovered from Irovetti has clarified the function of the enormous spiral seal upon Candlemere Island.

Evidence recovered or confirmed:

• Coins and remains associated with the Age of Destiny.

• An Aklo inscription translated as “we are those who stand at the threshold.”

• A colossal spiral seal associated with Pharasma.

• Conflicting references to individuals or entities named Foras and Korog, possibly brothers.

• Evidence that Pharasmian worshippers sealed themselves in the chambers below when the ward was created.

The seal prevents both physical and dimensional passage into the chambers beneath Candlemere. Possible methods of opening it include destruction by force, magical dispelling, or a purpose-designed ritual. Force may release the contents without warning. Dispelling could produce severe magical backlash. A ritual is the preferred method but has not yet been identified.

Assessment: Irovetti believed something below Candlemere could become a powerful ally. His judgment is not evidence of the prisoner’s loyalty, but it is evidence that the contents may be intelligent, powerful, and capable of affecting events beyond the island.

Minognos-Ushad

The immense pale wyvern sighted over the Glenebon mountains has been identified as Minognos-Ushad, the Eater of Kings. Tiger Lord stories place its lair farther west in the Branthlend Mountains.

The creature did not attack. Its apparent size and reputation suggest that engaging it without preparation would be exceptionally dangerous. Record its range, question Tiger Lord survivors where possible, and do not mount an expedition solely to confirm whether its title is deserved.

Hill-Giant Deserters

Four hill giants formerly attached to Kob Moleg’s defeated forces were encountered in the Glenebon uplands. Two were killed and two escaped. Recovered armbands confirmed their prior allegiance.

Assessment: Additional deserters may remain dispersed through the region. They lack central command but may turn to raiding as supplies diminish. Settlements and survey parties near the western routes should receive warning.

Fey Hunter Engagement

The party encountered a fey hunter and two supernatural hounds within a warm flowering glade during winter. The hunter stated that her kind were preparing the region for the coming Hunt and treated the party as prey. Seamus issued a challenge and combat followed.

The hounds called forth packs of misty attackers. The hunter repeatedly vanished and reappeared across the glade, concealed herself from sight, endured wounds that would have stopped an ordinary opponent, and concentrated several attacks upon Ally. Elowyn answered with an unusually powerful lance of divine magic.

Both hounds yielded. The hunter then offered surrender. Ally attacked after the surrender, believing it might be a trick and acting while wounded and angry. The hunter and both hounds escaped.

Before departing, the hunter stated that the fey would learn what occurred and would no longer offer mercy to the party.

Assessment: Treat the threat as credible. Fey customs concerning challenges, surrender, hospitality, and vengeance often operate as binding rules rather than rhetoric. The incident may affect negotiations with otherwise neutral fey and may be deliberately invoked by the Wild Hunt to isolate the Republic from potential First World allies.

Recommended action: Ask Melianse, Evindra, or another trusted fey intermediary whether restitution, formal apology, geas, offering, or trial could limit the consequences. Ally should seek counsel before participating in any future negotiation involving surrendered enemies.

Subsequent Development

Reports collected in the weeks following this encounter indicate that fey throughout the Republic have begun withdrawing customary warnings, guidance, shelter, and other informal assistance from mortal travelers. No coordinated campaign has been identified, but several deaths may already have resulted from this change in behavior.

Read the Supplemental Intelligence Report: Recent Fey Activity Within the Republic

Fitzroy, Ovinrbaane, and Brevoy

Fitzroy resigned after concluding that the party’s conduct and Ovinrbaane’s influence were changing him into someone incompatible with his faith. He returned to Brevoy with his household.

Ovinrbaane remains an intelligent, blood-hungry artifact that is slowly transforming Fitzroy into a likeness of Armag, the ancient Tiger Lord warlord. His departure removes the sword from the immediate council but does not contain it. It places the artifact in Brevoy, beyond the Republic’s routine observation.

Recommended action: Maintain discreet contact with Fitzroy and his household. Do not attempt to seize Ovinrbaane without preparation or consent. Seek additional research into separating bearer and artifact, reversing the physical transformation, and resisting the sword’s compulsions.

Ekundayo has accepted appointment as Minister of Defense. His transition should include current army readiness, western threat assessments, known fey routes, and all reports concerning the Wild Hunt.

Elowyn’s Church and the Tears of Razmir

Elowyn’s independent Razmiri church has continued to grow. Her new red vestments trimmed in gold and gold mask indicate advancement in rank or authority within the faith.

An underground trade in Tears of Razmir is operating through persons associated with the church. The substance is addictive. It has not been established whether Elowyn directs the trade, tolerates it, benefits from it without understanding its extent, or is being used by another faction within the congregation.

Assessment: A public accusation without evidence could drive the operation underground, create a religious confrontation, and prevent identification of its organizers. Quietly identify suppliers, distributors, affected citizens, financial routes, and any relationship between the drug and Elowyn’s new rank.

Recommended action: The Ministries of Intelligence and Justice should coordinate the investigation. Elowyn should not be confronted until the chain of supply and her degree of knowledge can be established.

Lurk and the Wild Hunt

On the morning of Abadius 4, 4716 AR, a hawk entered the council chamber and transformed into a grotesque leshy calling himself Lurk. He described himself as a guardian of the Stolen Lands.

Lurk warned that the Wild Hunt is approaching and that the spiral seal at Candlemere is failing. He insisted the seal must be repaired before the Hunt reaches the Republic.

No physical or magical examination has independently confirmed deterioration of the seal. Until such an examination is completed, Lurk’s claim should be treated as a credible warning rather than an established condition.

The relationship between the Hunt and the imprisoned presence remains unknown. Possibilities include:

  1. The Hunt intends to free the prisoner.

  2. The prisoner’s awakening is drawing the Hunt.

  3. The failing seal and the Hunt are separate symptoms of weakening boundaries with the First World.

  4. Lurk is manipulating the Republic into strengthening or opening the seal for purposes of his own.

Immediate priorities:

  1. Confirm Lurk’s identity and history through independent fey sources.

  2. Locate a ritual capable of examining or repairing the Candlemere seal without opening it.

  3. Prepare cold-iron arms, mobile shelters, signal plans, and evacuation routes for western settlements.

  4. Determine whether the Wild Hunt can be diverted, bargained with, challenged under fey law, or denied passage.

  5. Review the fey hunter incident before any emissary is sent. Assume the Hunt already knows what occurred.

Editor’s private note: I married Seamus beneath an open sky and believed, for one uninterrupted hour, that the future had agreed to wait its turn. It had not. It never does. But the vows were real, the warning is real, and we will have to be equal to both.

VOWS, FAREWELLS, AND THE COMING HUNT

Seamus and Linzi are wed, Mairi returns from Sevenarches, a minister resigns, and a warning reaches the Republic from the western wilds.

By Mara Venn, Managing Editor • Additional reporting by Darya Holt and Sella Copperclaw

This edition begins with a wedding and ends with a warning.

On Rova 21, citizens and guests gathered in Thumpington to witness the marriage of Seamus and Linzi. There was a tournament, a feast, a roast of unusual severity, and sufficient food to convince several visiting dignitaries that Thumping Waters conducts diplomacy primarily through baked goods.

During the months covered by this edition, however, the Republic also received news of a beloved performer’s death, welcomed Mairi home from a perilous journey, and accepted the resignation of Fitzroy Fulgrim III as Minister of Defense.

Now a strange messenger has arrived from the west. His warning is neither cheerful nor complicated: the Wild Hunt is coming.

TWO RIVERS JOINED IN THUMPINGTON

By Sella Copperclaw, Trade and Civic Affairs Correspondent

The marriage of Seamus and Linzi on Rova 21 transformed Thumpington into a city of ribbons, music, visiting relatives, improvised speeches, and people insisting they had always known this would happen.

The public celebration began with a tournament and continued through a feast large enough to require cooperation among kitchens throughout the capital. A traditional cookie table drew contributions from across the Republic. The resulting display was impressive enough that the Ministry of Treasury has declined to estimate its total value in butter.

The celebrations were not confined to the ceremony itself. Linzi’s companions marked her final unmarried evening at the Roc’s Omelette, while Seamus endured a Stag Night presided over by Annamede Belavara. Belavara used the occasion to roast the groom, his companions, and most of the government. Witnesses disagree about which suffered the greatest damage.

Velka officiated the wedding according to the river imagery of Hanspur. She spoke not of two lives becoming fixed, but of two waters joining while continuing to move and change.

Your waters are now combined, your rivers joined. So may you never demand that the other remain unchanged, and may you have courage to change together.

— Velka

Ally delivered a toast on behalf of the government and the friends who had watched the couple’s story unfold. Seamus, in turn, used part of the celebration to recognize Linzi not only as his bride, but as the chronicler whose work has preserved the Republic’s history from its beginning.

Seamus and Linzi exchange vows before Velka, priestess of Hanspur.

After the feast, Seamus and Linzi departed for a honeymoon in Absalom. The Lantern and Ledger’s editor has since returned to her desk and reports that the City at the Center of the World contains excellent printers, terrible filing practices, and several bookstores she was physically prevented from moving into.

HOW THE ROC’S OMELETTE GOT ITS NAME

By Sella Copperclaw, Trade and Civic Affairs Correspondent

Long before the Roc’s Omelette became one of Thumping Waters’ most fashionable establishments, it began with Jamery Gerbasken asking the government a perfectly reasonable question: could the Tubthumpers bring him one intact roc egg?

Gerbasken was already known for culinary enthusiasms that less adventurous cooks might describe as obsessions. His ambition was to prepare an entire omelette from a single roc egg and invite the republic’s finest cooks to compete over what should be put inside it.

Unfortunately, roc eggs are not commonly available from respectable grocers. The Tubthumpers traveled to the ruined watchtower atop Talon Peak, where they found three eggs, one extremely protective mother, and several hundred feet of regrettable open air. Antson Crazyfoot and Matteo were carried aloft during the resulting battle. The roc was knocked from the sky, Ant fell with it, and Mairi dove after him. One intact egg—reported to weigh approximately two hundred pounds—was eventually brought home with the assistance of a Shrink Item spell.

Gerbasken’s competition exceeded every expectation. Cooks arrived bearing cheeses, peppers, herbs, sausages, and culinary opinions strong enough to require official mediation. Spectators and merchants followed. The celebration brought such prosperity that Gerbasken was able to establish a luxury tavern without drawing upon the republic’s construction reserves.

Thus was born the Roc’s Omelette, which recently hosted Linzi’s final unmarried evening before her wedding to Seamus. The original omelette is long gone, but its consequences remain very much on the menu.

Gerbasken assures diners that no current dish contains genuine roc egg. Acquiring another, he explains, would be financially irresponsible, ecologically questionable, and extremely hard on the roof.

THE SUN STILL SHINES

From the Editor’s Desk

Three weeks before the wedding, word reached Thumpington that Leroy—musician, farmer, awakened rooster, and the performer known across Andoran simply as the King—had died quietly near Falcon’s Hollow.

The news arrived after his burial. Those who had known him gathered privately, lit candles, and remembered the artist whose final public performance had taken place during Thumpington’s Victory Festival. Ally read aloud the obituary subsequently published by this newspaper.

May the lamps keep on burning long after I’m gone.

— Leroy

The complete memorial editorial may be read in The Sun Still Shines.

The lamps remain lit.

MAIRI COMES HOME

By Darya Holt, Traveling Correspondent • From interviews conducted after her return

Mairi returned to Thumpington on Desnus 7 following a dangerous pilgrimage to Sevenarches undertaken to free the spirit trapped within Amiri’s greatsword.

The sword had been pursued by a malevolent ghost and burdened by a spirit that could not escape it without powerful aid. The precise rite performed in Sevenarches was difficult, dangerous, and successful. Mairi returned alive. The imprisoned spirit did not return with her.

Afterward, Mairi searched for Amiri, the surviving Six Bears, and the Tiger Lords, but found no certain trace of them. She brought the cleansed sword back to Thumpington and placed it above the mantle in her home.

Mairi lays Amiri’s sword to rest following her return from Sevenarches.

There are weapons displayed as trophies, weapons retained for future wars, and weapons kept because forgetting them would be a second betrayal. Those who have seen the sword above Mairi’s hearth understand which kind it is.

Having concluded that final obligation, Mairi announced her retirement from public adventuring. She has settled with Euan and intends to enjoy some portion of the peace she helped create.

Those who know Mairi may define “retirement” according to their own judgment. Citizens encountering any monster inclined to test the matter are advised to stand well clear.

RESEARCH CONTINUES AT CANDLEMERE

By Darya Holt, Traveling Correspondent

During the months surrounding the wedding, government researchers continued examining the ancient ruins upon Candlemere Island. The structure is believed to be more than five thousand years old and contains evidence of Pharasmian activity from an otherwise poorly documented period in the region’s history.

Several inscriptions repeat an Aklo phrase previously discovered within the ruins:

We are those who stand at the threshold.

The site remains closed to unauthorized exploration. Citizens are reminded that ancient ruins containing untranslated warnings are not suitable destinations for fishing trips, amateur treasure hunts, religious pilgrimages, or dares made after closing time at a tavern.

REPORTS FROM THE GLENEBON UPLANDS

By Darya Holt, Traveling Correspondent

On Neth 4, the ministers resumed their survey of the Glenebon Uplands following a period of travel, study, retraining, and governance. The expedition confirmed that the western mountains remain spectacular, strategically troublesome, and openly hostile to road construction.

The expedition crossed fields of sulfurous water, boiling geysers, and springs acidic enough to discourage even the most optimistic civil engineer. Several travelers suffered burns when the ground vented without warning. No permanent injuries were reported.

Farther into the mountains, the company sighted an immense pale wyvern circling above the peaks. Ally identified the creature as Minognos-Ushad, called the Eater of Kings in Tiger Lord stories. The legendary beast is believed to lair farther west in the Branthlend Mountains. It did not attack, and the expedition did not attempt to improve upon that arrangement.

The survey later encountered four hill giants believed to have deserted the defeated forces of Kob Moleg. Two were killed in the ensuing clash and two escaped. Weapons and armbands recovered from the dead confirmed their former allegiance.

Travelers entering the uplands are advised to avoid steaming ground, watch the skies, and remember that a road marked “unsuitable” on a government survey is not thereby transformed into an exciting shortcut.

A CHANGE AT THE MINISTRY OF DEFENSE

By Mara Venn, Managing Editor

Following the western expedition, Fitzroy Fulgrim III resigned as Minister of Defense and returned to Brevoy with his household. He cited personal and religious considerations and concluded that he could no longer discharge the duties of the office in good conscience.

Fitzroy served the Republic through war, expansion, and some of the most dangerous expeditions ever undertaken by its ministers. His departure should not erase that service, and The Lantern and Ledger wishes him peace upon the road ahead.

At Fitzroy’s recommendation, Ekundayo has assumed the office of Minister of Defense.

Ekundayo assumes the duties of Minister of Defense following Fitzroy Fulgrim III’s resignation.

Ekundayo brings years of experience as a hunter, soldier, magistrate, and defender of the Republic to the office. His appointment provides continuity as the government reviews the defenses of its western settlements.

THE WILD HUNT IS COMING

By Mara Venn, Managing Editor

This morning, the council received a credible warning of increased fey activity across the Stolen Lands and the possible approach of a phenomenon known as the Wild Hunt.

The government is reviewing defenses, consulting experts, and coordinating with communities near the western frontier. At present, there is no order to evacuate any settlement. Citizens should nevertheless report unusual gatherings of animals, unseasonable changes in weather or vegetation, unexplained hunting horns, and unfamiliar figures travelling with fey creatures.

Do not challenge such figures. Do not accept invitations to join a hunt. Do not provide your true name, promise a favor, drink from an offered cup, follow music into the forest, or assume that a conversation has ended simply because the other participant has vanished.

The Wild Hunt has not yet reached our settlements. The warning has been received, and the Republic intends to be ready if it does.

FROM THE EDITOR’S DESK

By Linzi, Editor and Minister of Communication

A wedding encourages people to speak about beginnings, which is understandable. Beginnings are attractive things: they arrive wearing clean clothes and have not yet accumulated revisions. But a marriage is not a beginning without a past. Seamus and I carried absent parents, old enemies, wars, songs, arguments, friends, and a shelf’s worth of poorly timed danger into our vows. We did not arrive empty. We arrived together.

The same is true of this Republic. Mairi returned carrying a sword that had finally released its dead. Fitzroy departed because the living had asked too much of his conscience. Leroy left us a song and then left the world quietly. A messenger has arrived to say that something old and terrible is hunting its way toward everything we have built. None of those stories begins here, and none of them is finished.

Velka told us that joined rivers must have the courage to change together. I have had several months now to discover that marriage does not make the future wait politely while two people learn how to be married. Countries receive no such courtesy either. The vows were made beneath an open sky, the work resumed afterward, and both were real.

That may be what I remember most about Rova 21. Not that the Republic stopped for our wedding, because it did not, but that so many people made room for joy while everything else continued moving around us. There are worse ways to understand a country. There are worse ways to enter a marriage.

The water ahead is dark, but Velka did not tell us to wait for still water. She told us to have the courage to change together. We will meet it moving.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication
