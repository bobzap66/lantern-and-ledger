Session 06: The Elk and the Boar

Session 06: The Elk and the Boar

Sep 18, 20267 min read

Side

:

From Linzi’s Journal

An account of the expedition from 24 Pharast through 1 Gozran, 4710 AR, reconstructed from the testimony of those present

When the party returned to Oleg’s Trading Post, they brought Svetlana the wedding ring recovered beneath The Old Sycamore. Its return earned them fifty gold pieces of credit at the trading post, although Svetlana’s relief seemed considerably more valuable. She turned the ring between her fingers for a long moment before saying, “I kept telling myself it was only a thing. But it was our thing.” Oleg placed an arm around her and glared at anyone who appeared in danger of remarking upon the tenderness of the moment.

The party also brought wounds, several new obligations, and Mairi’s estrangement from the Wolf spirit. Mairi spent an entire day in prayer and atonement, asking forgiveness for having violated the spirit’s demands during her rage. The Wolf accepted her apology, and its presence returned to her. I do not know what passed between them during those hours, but when Mairi rose, the terrible loneliness I had seen in her was gone.

Amiri had wandered into Oleg’s during the party’s absence. It did not take long for conversation between her and Mairi to become a contest. After an exchange that neither woman would describe as friendly, Mairi wagered a magical weapon that the party would kill the notorious boar Tuskgutter before Amiri could. Amiri accepted with the sort of grin ordinarily seen immediately before an axe begins moving. This transformed the hunt from a task worth attempting into a matter of immediate and apparently existential importance.

Before they could set out, a druid of Erastil named Jhod Kavken told them of a vision. “The Elk Father showed me a shrine swallowed by the forest,” he explained. “Something wounded waits there, unable to find peace. The temple must be found, and what has corrupted it must be cleansed.” Somewhere within the Narlmarches stood this lost temple, and Jhod believed the party had been brought to him so that they might restore it. They agreed to search for the shrine while pursuing Tuskgutter. On 24 Pharast, after placing an order for supplies from Restov, Seamus, Mairi, Ant, and Ally departed Oleg’s Trading Post.

After two days of travel, they stumbled upon the Temple of the Elk. Time and wilderness had nearly reclaimed it, leaving broken stone, encroaching vegetation, and a great fallen tree lying across the surrounding ruins. As the party began exploring, the temple’s ancient bear guardian attacked. The creature fought with the strength of something driven by more than hunger, and Ant was nearly killed during the struggle.

Pyjat emerged from the nearby forest and began healing the wounded. Her intervention gave the others the time they needed to recover and bring down the guardian. With the bear’s death, the spirit bound to the temple was finally released from its suffering. The party had found Jhod’s lost shrine and begun the work of cleansing it, although there was little time to linger. Somewhere to the south, Tuskgutter remained alive, and every hour spent resting was another hour in which Amiri might reach him first.

On 27 Pharast, the party located Tuskgutter’s lair. They chose their ground carefully around a fallen tree and spent hours preparing an ambush. Tuskgutter returned, surveyed their clever arrangements, and demonstrated precisely how much respect an enormous enraged boar possesses for careful planning. He charged Ally immediately and tore into her with his tusks.

Seamus leapt from the fallen tree, landed upon Tuskgutter’s back, and attempted to grapple him. This was an act of remarkable courage, astonishing confidence, and highly questionable judgment. Tuskgutter responded by turning his tusks upon Seamus and nearly killing him outright. Pyjat’s aid kept him alive, while Ally, Ant, and Mairi worked desperately to finish the boar before it could complete the task.

They succeeded. Tuskgutter fell, leaving the party with his enormous head, a settled wager, and another story in which Seamus survived something he had no reasonable expectation of surviving. I am beginning to suspect that he does these things specifically to make my accounts more dramatic. If so, I appreciate the consideration and wish he would stop.

With Tuskgutter’s head in tow, the party continued south toward a patch of fangberries they had promised to harvest for Bokken. The berries were guarded by nests of aggressive spiders, which swarmed over the party when they entered the thicket. Venom, bleeding, and a great deal of frantic swatting followed. The party eventually drove the swarms away and gathered the fangberries, although I am told that nobody felt particularly enthusiastic about eating anything resembling a berry afterward.

Rather than retrace their route, the party explored a different path north toward Oleg’s. On 29 Pharast, they encountered a strange boggard living alone among a pair of ruined buildings. Neither side could understand the other, but after some careful observation and restrained gestures, the party concluded that the boggard wished only to be left alone. Having learned something from their encounter with the frost giant, they respected that wish and continued on without violence.

The following day, they discovered a brush thylacine trapped at the bottom of a pit. It was a vicious creature and unlikely to show gratitude, but Mairi refused to leave it there to starve. Seamus used magic to put the animal to sleep while the others lowered a ladder made from brush into the pit. Once the thylacine had a means of escape, the party mounted their horses and fled north before it awoke and reconsidered their relationship. Mairi’s compassion did not go unnoticed. Gozreh’s favor shone upon her for preserving a creature of the wild, even one capable of repaying kindness with teeth.

On 31 Pharast, the party’s return journey was interrupted by giant frogs occupying a hot spring. The disagreement was brief and violent, although after boars, bears, spiders, and thylacines, the frogs seem to have inspired more irritation than terror.

On the first day of Gozran, they discovered the body of a trapper. Examination suggested that the man had been murdered rather than killed by an animal or an accident. The party wondered whether he might be Breeg Orlivanch, a widely disliked trapper who had been missing for nearly a month, but they found too little evidence to be certain. With Tuskgutter’s head and Bokken’s fangberries both threatening to become considerably less presentable, they marked the location and returned to Oleg’s.

They arrived carrying a monstrous boar’s head, a basket of battered berries, news of a restored temple, and questions concerning a murdered man. Seamus was bruised, bandaged, and altogether too pleased with himself. I was relieved to see him alive and furious that he had once again made the matter uncertain. Mairi, meanwhile, had regained the Wolf, won her wager, and earned Gozreh’s favor through an act of compassion. For an expedition begun in competition, it had revealed something important about her: she might rage like a beast, but she would not willingly leave one to suffer.
