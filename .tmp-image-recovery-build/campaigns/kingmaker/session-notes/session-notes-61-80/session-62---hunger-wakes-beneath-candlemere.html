Session 62: Hunger Wakes Beneath Candlemere

Session 62: Hunger Wakes Beneath Candlemere

Ally’s force magic tears into the shoggoth while Seamus remains trapped inside its living mass. Reconstruction from the expedition’s account.

Republic Chronicle — Restricted

Compiled for the council by Linzi. Abadius 21, 4716 AR.

The expedition beneath Candlemere has confirmed that the old complex is not merely a ruin. The markings describe the inverse Spiral of Yog-Sothoth and a ninefold Helix Theorem intended to close a symbolic gate from its center outward. The name Foras appears in the surviving record. Treat all three matters as restricted until the council has determined what, if anything, remains active.

The defenders encountered below are Thresholders: hermeticists, disciples, and mystics preserving an older cultic purpose. Their bodies and equipment suggest that some were held outside ordinary time. One mystic displayed magic that postponed the consequence of what should have been a fatal mace blow from Elowyn. The injury did not take effect immediately, allowing the mystic time for one final attack before collapsing.

A separate hazard was a shoggoth. It spoke through the party’s minds, repeating “Hungry! Hungry!” and “Tekeli-li.” Seamus remained inside it until Ally’s force magic destroyed the creature. Ally’s overcharged translation wand then exploded. The expedition’s account records the use of ten earplugs and a strong restorative draught before the creature was brought down.

The scriptorium contained a stone tablet that produces a moving illusion, a powerful summoning scroll, and a tome that crumbled when Matteo touched it. Ally identified the resulting residue as King’s Sleep, a slow and virulent poison. The party also recovered the inverse Spiral and Helix notes. Do not release these details.

The expedition has reached a bunk room occupied by several surviving disciples. They rose together and shouted in Hallit. The party has not yet determined whether they are prisoners, sentries, or both.

Abadius 21, 4716 AR · Published in Thumpington · Linzi, Editor · Mara Venn, Managing Editor · Price 1 cp

The following account has been prepared from the expedition’s report, corroborating testimony, and the observations of the Republic’s appointed scholars. Where the evidence remains incomplete, we have said so.

HUNGER WAKES BENEATH CANDLEMERE

Expedition finds a wailing dead, a voice in the dark, and a second door still closed

By Darya Holt, Special Correspondent

The party sent beneath Lake Candlemere has returned from another day in the island’s sealed chambers. They brought back no map that can make the place seem sensible, but they did bring back evidence that the island’s dangers are not confined to its shoreline.

Their first discovery was a dead elf who did not remain quiet. The figure rose as a banshee in an angled chamber whose walls are crowded with carvings of crushing stones, spiked prison grates, and mourners beside dead trees. Its cry carried through the stone with enough force to stop a less prepared expedition. The party’s cleric, Elowyn Vask, answered the apparition with healing magic turned against the undead. Her final burst of magic restored badly injured companions and ended the haunting.

Linzi’s later research identifies the banshee as Kyetriel, a famed elven mercenary who is believed to have marched with Kellid forces against the cultists during the island’s earlier history. The identification is not yet public knowledge in any official sense, but it gives the corpse a name and the old battle a witness. The party recovered fine elven chain, an enchanted elven curve blade, and a cloak woven with protective elven magic from the remains.

Kyetriel’s last stand in the Hall of the Relinquished. The chamber and its accumulated wall carvings are reconstructed from the expedition’s account; Kyetriel’s appearance is based on her surviving elven equipment.

THE ROOM OF BOOKS DID NOT CONTAIN BOOKS FOR LONG

The next chamber was a scriptorium: brass, stone, and paper arranged for a culture that expected its readers to understand diagrams before sentences. The expedition recovered a powerful summoning scroll and a tablet that produces a moving illusion when read. A more ordinary-looking volume proved less cooperative. It collapsed into dust at a touch, leaving the party to identify its residue rather than its title.

The expedition’s report is careful here, and so are we. It is possible to mistake an old archive for a collection of valuables when the archive has survived by making every object difficult to handle. The safest conclusion is that the scriptorium was a working place, not a vault.

“HUNGRY! HUNGRY!”

In a cavern lit by fungus, the party met the thing that supplied this issue’s headline. The creature’s voice arrived in their minds before its body could be properly seen, repeating “Hungry! Hungry!” as if appetite were the only fact it had left. The first exchange confused several members of the expedition, and the creature engulfed Seamus and Matteo before Matteo cut a path free.

Seamus did not escape under his own power. He remained inside until Ally Grainger’s force magic destroyed the creature around him. The wand Ally used to translate the creature’s other utterance—“Tekeli-li”—then overcharged and exploded. The expedition’s account records the use of ten earplugs and a considerable quantity of restorative drink. Candlemere remains, as ever, an expensive place to learn a word.

A FATAL BLOW, DELAYED

The party’s most difficult fight took place in an echoing laboratory. A silent disciple stood beside a mystic whose defenses were less obvious than they first appeared. Elowyn Vask landed a crushing mace blow that should have ended the fight immediately. Instead, the mystic’s magic held the consequence at bay while the wound itself remained horribly apparent.

The reprieve lasted only long enough for the mystic to make one final attack. Then the postponed injury took effect and the defender fell. One witness summarized the exchange afterward with the words, “See, Razmir fixed it.” The line has been repeated often enough to deserve printing, though not often enough to make the underlying magic less alarming.

Two moments in the Echo Laboratory: Elowyn’s fatal mace blow is held outside the mystic’s body, then comes due after the defender makes one final attack. Reconstruction from the expedition’s combat report.

The expedition later encountered another group of disciples and a hermeticist. Ally raised a wall of fire while Seamus repeatedly forced the surviving attackers through it. The method was direct, labor-intensive, and effective—the three qualities most likely to survive a committee meeting.

THE MIRRORS KEEP THEIR OWN RECORD

The route back toward the island’s central chambers passed through a hall of mirrors. The reflections were not simple copies. Attended objects appeared where their owners stood, while unattended doors and benches failed to appear at all. Ally’s reflection showed tusks.

No one has yet determined whether the mirrors reveal a concealed nature, a possible one, or merely the preference of whatever made the hall. The expedition wisely declined to conduct a second survey for the sake of a cleaner answer.

Ally’s reflection preserves every visible detail of her clothing and equipment while adding two prominent tusks. Reconstruction from the expedition’s account.

PUBLIC NOTICE: CANDLEMERE REMAINS CLOSED

Candlemere Island and the waters immediately surrounding it remain closed to unauthorized travel by order of the council.

This restriction includes fishing, salvage, religious pilgrimage, private magical investigation, treasure hunting, sightseeing, and attempts to determine whether objects thrown into reported spatial anomalies can later be recovered.

Boat owners are asked to report attempted charters to the nearest watch office. No reward is presently offered for proving that the rumors are true.

FROM THE EDITOR’S DESK

By Linzi, Editor and Minister of Communication

The island has given us a wailing dead, a hungry voice, and a room full of mirrors that cannot agree on what a person is. None of these discoveries makes the water safer. All of them make the council’s closure easier to defend.

There is a temptation, when a dangerous place yields a name or a useful object, to call the danger understood. We should resist it. A name is not an explanation. A recovered weapon is not a victory. Even a report that ends with the expedition alive is only a report that ends with the expedition alive.

Candlemere remains closed. The party has gone back in. We will print what can be verified, correct what cannot, and leave the island its secrets until someone can bring them home without also bringing the island with them.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication
