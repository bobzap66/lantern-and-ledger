Fort Drelev

At the northwestern edge of Hooktongue Slough stands Fort Drelev, a fortified western settlement of the Republic of Thumping Waters. Founded during Brevoy’s effort to establish new realms in the Stolen Lands, the town spent years as the seat of Baron Hannis Drelev before his government collapsed beneath war, occupation, and rebellion.

Hannis is dead. The barony is gone. Fort Drelev retained its walls, its keep, and even the name of the man who once ruled it, but its political order has been rebuilt around a different principle: the settlement is no longer the possession of the household living above it.

At a Glance

Nation: Republic of Thumping Waters

Region: Northwestern Hooktongue Slough

Government: Republican administration under Steward Terrion Numesti

Keep: Administered by Lady Pavetta Stroon-Drelev, Castellan of Drelev Keep

Former Ruler: Baron Hannis Drelev

Principal Roles: Western fortress, trade settlement, military post, and recovery center for the former Drelev barony

Notable Landmark: The Velvet Corner

Founding

Fort Drelev began with one of the charters issued during Brevoy’s attempt to tame the Stolen Lands. Hannis Drelev, a younger son of a Brevic noble house, saw the venture as an opportunity to establish a domain of his own. His wife, Pavetta Stroon-Drelev, brought wealth and social connections to the project, while her brother, Imeckas Stroon, supplied arcane expertise and political counsel.

The settlement’s position near Hooktongue Slough was strategically useful but difficult. Drelev Keep and the town’s fortifications received early investment, while the civilian settlement developed more unevenly around them. The enormous temple of Erastil, intended to become one of Fort Drelev’s great public buildings, remained unfinished even as the keep above it grew increasingly comfortable.

That imbalance became emblematic of the barony Hannis built: strong walls surrounding a government whose priorities were far less durable.

The Drelev Barony

Under Hannis, Fort Drelev became sharply divided between the keep and the Commons. Taxes and tolls were aggressively collected, mercenaries increasingly dominated public life, and businesses were expected to support soldiers whether or not payment followed. Travel restrictions and curfews tightened as Hannis’s political position worsened.

By the final months of his rule, the civilian settlement was visibly deteriorating. Shops had closed, horses had been stripped from local stables for the Tiger Lords, and hill giants served alongside mercenaries as instruments of intimidation. The Bellwether Soup Kettle struggled to feed its own household while Drelev’s guards ate there without paying. The Iron Steeple Inn stood nearly abandoned, its windows broken and much of its value already taken.

Hannis’s greatest failure was not merely cruelty but capitulation. Faced with pressure from Pitax and the Tiger Lords, he protected his own position by sacrificing the security of the people beneath him. Noble hostages, including Tamary Numesti, were surrendered as tribute. When Lord Terrion Numesti opposed that decision, Hannis imprisoned him beneath Drelev Keep.

War with Thumping Waters

Relations with Thumping Waters collapsed into open war after Hannis aligned himself with Pitax and the Tiger Lords. An army led by his mercenary captain, Aemon Trask, invaded the Republic and attacked Tatzlford. The assault failed, and Trask fled the battlefield.

Soon afterward, Kisandra Numesti reached the Tubthumpers and asked for their help. Her appeal went beyond rescuing her father and sister. Fort Drelev’s people had spent too long believing resistance was impossible; removing Hannis without breaking that fear would leave the conditions that sustained his rule largely intact.

Inside the town, Satinder Morne had reached much the same conclusion. From The Velvet Corner, she became the Tubthumpers’ principal contact in Fort Drelev and urged them to weaken Hannis’s authority without turning the Commons into a battlefield. Aid the townsfolk. Remove isolated patrols. Defeat the giants enforcing the curfew. Let people see that Drelev’s servants could bleed.

The first major public demonstration came at the Iron Steeple Inn, where the Tubthumpers killed one of Hannis’s hill giants. The battle nearly burned the abandoned inn to the ground, but the building survived. More importantly, so did the witnesses.

The Velvet Corner

The Velvet Corner was Fort Drelev’s gambling hall, brothel, drinking establishment, and—quietly—one of the most important resistance sites in the settlement. Satinder Morne kept the business open while mercenaries and guards filled its public rooms, using their confidence and loose tongues to gather information while concealing people working against the baron.

When the Tubthumpers entered Fort Drelev, the Corner became their safehouse. Satinder provided hidden rooms, healing, local intelligence, and directions to the secret escape tunnel leading from Drelev Keep toward Lake Hooktongue. Her advice shaped the broader strategy of resistance: undermine the regime carefully enough that civilians did not pay for their own liberation twice.

The Corner’s history matters because Fort Drelev’s fall was not simply a military event. It depended upon people inside the town who had survived Hannis long enough to know where his government was weakest.

The Fall of Hannis Drelev

The final attack on Drelev Keep began with an act of relief rather than war. Before entering the keep, Velka taught Ally the magic needed to create food, and the Tubthumpers distributed it among Fort Drelev’s hungry children.

Using the secret tunnel revealed by Satinder, the company entered the caverns beneath the keep, fought through splitting oozes, and reached the dungeons. There they freed Terrion Numesti, who armed himself from one of the fallen guards and provided a detailed account of the keep before leaving to recover and make plans for Tamary’s rescue.

The Tubthumpers’ attempt to move invisibly through the occupied keep failed when guards detected them in the ground-floor foyer. One fled for reinforcements, with Seamus pursuing him through the keep until the guard burst into Pavetta Stroon-Drelev’s chambers and died there.

The encounter that followed became a negotiation. Pavetta and Imeckas had no desire to die for Hannis and offered cooperation in exchange for preserving Pavetta’s position. Velka rejected any arrangement that simply replaced Hannis while leaving the same household above the same people.

Imeckas nevertheless provided useful intelligence. Hannis was dining with Aemon Trask, Lady Quintessa Maray, and several trusted guards. The Tubthumpers attacked. Hannis, Trask, and three guards were killed. Quintessa escaped by teleportation.

The death of the baron ended Hannis Drelev’s government. It did not make the settlement’s next government automatic.

The New Administration

The settlement’s recovery was organized around a deliberate separation between civic government and the old noble household.

Steward Terrion Numesti became Fort Drelev’s principal civic officer during reconstruction. His responsibilities include relations with Thumping Waters, restoration of public order, recovery of local businesses, oversight of public works, and the long process of rebuilding trust between residents and the institutions that once failed them. The title of Steward reflects the provisional nature of that work: Terrion administers Fort Drelev on behalf of its people and the Republic rather than claiming the town as a personal domain.

Lady Pavetta Stroon-Drelev, widow of the late baron, retains the courtesy title of Lady and now serves as Castellan of Drelev Keep. Her authority is confined principally to the keep, its household, maintenance, stores, accommodations, and coordination with the Republican administration. She does not govern Fort Drelev, levy taxes, command the town’s civil institutions, or inherit Hannis’s barony.

The arrangement preserves a place for Pavetta without pretending her previous privilege entitled her to continue ruling the people who suffered beneath her husband’s government. It also gives the keep a responsible resident administrator whose comfort now depends upon keeping the building useful to the settlement rather than isolated from it.

Imeckas Stroon remains valuable enough to tolerate and inconvenient enough to supervise. His arcane knowledge, familiarity with the keep, and willingness to cooperate have kept him within the new order, though neither intelligence nor family connection grants him independent political authority.

Recovery and Thumping Waters

After Hannis’s death, Imeckas supplied information about the Tiger Lords and the hostages surrendered to them. The Tubthumpers subsequently rescued Tamary Numesti and four other noble hostages, eliminating one of the last immediate consequences of Hannis’s capitulation.

During the spring of 4714 AR, Fort Drelev formally pledged itself to Thumping Waters. Republican defenses extended westward, trade and military routes were reorganized, and the settlement began recovering from the combined damage of misrule, occupation, war, and isolation.

The process has been uneven. The keep survived largely intact, but that is not the same thing as saying Fort Drelev did. Shops must reopen, homes require repair, roads and drainage need attention, and institutions must convince residents accustomed to arbitrary authority that rules now bind officials as well as everyone else.

Fort Drelev Today

Drelev Keep still dominates the skyline, but it no longer defines the entire settlement. The Commons remain the place where Fort Drelev’s recovery is most visible: repaired storefronts, reopened kitchens, occupied rooms, restored stables, and streets in which a sudden knock no longer automatically means soldiers have arrived to take something.

The unfinished temple of Erastil still stands as an awkward monument to the old government’s priorities. Whether it is eventually completed, repurposed, or left partially unfinished remains a civic question rather than a baron’s vanity project.

Fort Drelev’s location also makes it increasingly important to the Republic. It sits between Hooktongue Slough, the western roads, Pitax, and the territories once threatened by the Tiger Lords. Soldiers, merchants, couriers, marsh guides, and travelers all pass through a settlement whose strategic value survived its ruler.

Notable Locations

Drelev Keep: Former seat of Hannis Drelev and now administered by Castellan Pavetta Stroon-Drelev. Its dungeons once held Terrion Numesti, and its banquet hall was the site of Hannis’s death. The keep remains the town’s strongest fortification but is now a public military and administrative asset rather than the center of an independent barony.

The Commons: Fort Drelev’s civilian heart. Under Hannis, its flooded streets, struggling businesses, and neglected homes stood in sharp contrast to the keep. Its recovery remains the clearest measure of whether liberation has improved ordinary life.

The Velvet Corner: Satinder Morne’s gambling hall, brothel, and drinking establishment. It served as a covert resistance safehouse and intelligence center during the campaign against Hannis.

Bellwether Soup Kettle: A modest local eatery whose owners endured systematic exploitation by Drelev’s guards. Its continued operation has become a small but useful symbol of the Commons reclaiming normal life.

Iron Steeple Inn: Once a respectable inn and nearly abandoned by the end of Hannis’s reign. A hill giant enforcing the curfew was killed inside during the uprising. Fire nearly destroyed the building during the battle, but it survived.

Temple of Erastil: A massive unfinished temple conceived as a major civic and religious institution. Its incomplete state remains one of the most visible reminders of a government that preferred appearances to completion.

Legacy

The name Fort Drelev has outlived the man who gave it that name. That fact is uncomfortable, but useful. The settlement does not need to erase its past in order to reject it.

Hannis tried to make Fort Drelev proof that he deserved to rule. Instead, its history became an argument against personal rule itself. His armies were defeated, the prisoner beneath his keep was freed, the hostages he surrendered were recovered, and the people he expected to remain frightened watched his enforcers fall one by one.

The walls remain. The keep remains. Even the name remains.

The barony does not.
