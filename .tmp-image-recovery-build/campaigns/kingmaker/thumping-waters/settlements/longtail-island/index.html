Longtail Island

Set in the divided current of the Shrike River, Longtail Island is the civic home of the Longtail iruxi and one of the Republic of Thumping Waters’ most important river settlements. What began as a fortified tribal refuge has grown into a busy island town of bridges, workshops, markets, barracks, docks, and public works without ceasing to be recognizably Longtail.

The settlement’s transformation is closely tied to Vesket, who remains a leader of the Longtail people while also serving as mayor within the Republic. Longtail Island did not become part of Thumping Waters by discarding its earlier identity. Its older customs, ancestral traditions, martial discipline, and river knowledge now exist beside republican offices, trade networks, civic infrastructure, and institutions built for a larger community.

From Island Stronghold to River Town

Before its integration into Thumping Waters, Longtail Island was a marshy fortified settlement defended by wooden stakes, palisades, and the river itself. The Longtails lived under Vesket’s rule and placed great weight upon strength, ancestry, and the guidance of the spirits they believed watched over them.

That trust was exploited by a will-o’-wisp posing as an ancestral presence. Under its influence, Vesket was driven toward fear, aggression, and choices that endangered his own people. The Tubthumpers’ intervention exposed the deception and broke the creature’s hold over the settlement.

What followed mattered more than the defeat itself. Vesket did not abandon the Longtails or their traditions. Instead, he accepted a new civic role within the growing Republic and directed the same discipline once used to defend an isolated stronghold toward roads, bridges, trade, public safety, and the long work of rebuilding.

Longtail Island was formally established as a settlement of Thumping Waters on 12 Neth 4710 AR. Its subsequent growth has been steady enough that visitors who remember the old marsh fort often struggle to reconcile it with the modern town.

Leadership

Vesket serves as Longtail Island’s mayor and remains a traditional leader among the Longtail iruxi. The distinction matters. His republican office governs the settlement’s civic relationship with Thumping Waters, while his authority among his own people reaches back to the community that existed before the Republic arrived.

Vesket’s leadership remains direct, disciplined, and strongly shaped by his past. He has little patience for waste, vanity, or authority that cannot justify itself through results. The humiliation of having once been manipulated by a false spirit made him more cautious rather than less commanding, and he now places far greater value upon tested counsel, practical competence, and institutions that can survive a bad leader.

The island’s administration draws upon both republican and Longtail traditions. Soldiers, river pilots, craftspeople, elders, and advisers all have a place in decisions affecting the bridges, docks, defenses, trade, and river traffic. Longtail Island’s government is not simply Thumpington reproduced with scales; it reflects the people who built it.

The Island Today

Longtail Island lies between two channels of the Shrike and is connected to the mainland by a pair of sturdy bridges. Reinforced embankments protect the busiest sections of shore, while walls, watch platforms, and a central watchtower give the settlement control over approaches by both road and water.

The old muddy tracks have largely given way to paved streets and raised working areas. Timber and clay houses stand beside workshops, storehouses, barracks, civic buildings, and river facilities. A town hall handles public administration, while the keep and garrison anchor the island’s defenses.

The settlement’s sewer and drainage systems are a particular source of local pride. That pride is not misplaced. Building reliable streets, foundations, waste channels, and water control on a river island required years of engineering and maintenance, and the resulting infrastructure is one of the clearest signs of Longtail Island’s transformation from a defensible refuge into a permanent town.

River Trade and Industry

Longtail Island thrives because the river passes through it. Barges, fishing craft, timber rafts, merchants, and travelers move constantly along the Shrike, and the island’s bridges and docks make it a natural place to stop, trade, repair, and resupply.

A lumberyard and mill process timber brought from upriver, while the municipal smithy handles routine tools, weapons, fittings, and repairs. Fishers, herbalists, pilots, builders, and craftspeople supply both the local population and passing traffic. Granaries and storehouses protect the settlement against disrupted trade and seasonal shortages.

The island’s most specialized workshop is Tailforge Atelier, the riverside forge of master smith Ssekesh. Tailforge produces fine tools, bridge hardware, weapons, ceremonial work, and watercraft fittings for customers throughout the Republic. Its current-powered bellows and fitting dock make the workshop inseparable from the river that powers Longtail Island’s economy.

Tailforge also reflects the island’s broader character. Traditional Longtail knowledge of river, weather, shell, bone, and survival sits beside imported engineering, specialized metallurgy, apprenticeship, and regional trade. The result is not one tradition replacing another, but both being tested against the needs of the work.

Defense and the Bridges

Longtail Island’s position makes it both a trading center and a strategic checkpoint. Its garrison patrols the bridges, walls, docks, and surrounding waterways, while watch posts monitor traffic moving along the Shrike.

The twin bridges are especially important. They connect the island to the mainland, carry merchants and travelers through the settlement, and give the local government direct responsibility for one of the Republic’s key river crossings. Tailforge and the municipal smithy both contribute hardware and repairs needed to keep those crossings usable.

Longtail military culture has not disappeared simply because the settlement now values commerce. The difference is one of purpose. Strength that once primarily defended the tribe from outsiders is now also used to protect travelers, public works, trade, and the wider river route.

Faith, Ancestors, and Memory

A central shrine honors both Hanspur and Gozreh, reflecting the island’s relationship with river, current, storm, and travel. The shared shrine is used by both Longtail and human worshippers and has become part of the settlement’s civic as well as religious landscape. Tailforge’s custom of carrying shrine water for its first quench each month is one example of how faith and ordinary craft overlap on the island.

Longtail ancestral traditions remain important as well. The settlement does not treat the deception that once ensnared Vesket as proof that ancestral belief itself was foolish. Instead, the experience left the community with a harder lesson: reverence does not excuse abandoning judgment.

The cemetery and memorial works on the island preserve the names of Longtail dead alongside others lost in later conflicts, including the Bloom. Tailforge has contributed commemorative fittings to the keep, shrine, cemetery, ancestral park, and the carved pillar honoring those dead.

Life on Longtail Island

Longtail Island remains predominantly iruxi, but merchants, craftspeople, soldiers, officials, and families from elsewhere in the Republic have made homes there as trade expanded. Common and Iruxi languages mix in the markets and workshops, and visitors encounter a settlement whose public life is unmistakably shaped by both river commerce and Longtail custom.

Festivals remain loud, disciplined affairs of drums, torches, food, contests, and communal display. The same people who maintain strict bridge watches and careful inventories are perfectly capable of celebrating hard enough for the sound to carry across both channels.

The island’s hospitality is practical. Travelers are welcome because travelers bring news, coin, goods, and relationships, but Longtail respect is earned through conduct rather than status. People who keep agreements, respect the river, and understand that public safety is everyone’s concern generally find the island easier to navigate than those who arrive expecting deference.

Place in the Republic

Longtail Island is one of the strongest examples of Thumping Waters expanding through incorporation rather than replacement. The Longtails did not need to stop being Longtails in order to become citizens, and Vesket did not need to become a different person before he could become an effective mayor.

That continuity gives the settlement unusual political weight. Longtail Island demonstrates that older forms of leadership and identity can survive inside the Republic when they can coexist with common law, civic responsibility, and shared institutions. The island’s bridges are therefore more than infrastructure. They are an accurate symbol for the settlement itself: built between two banks without requiring either one to disappear.

Economically, the town remains a vital link in the Shrike River network connecting Olegton, Fort Tuskwater, the capital, and the southern reaches of the Republic. Politically, it proves that reconciliation is not the same thing as assimilation. Longtail Island became stronger by joining something larger without pretending it had begun there.

From the Journal of Linzi, Minister of Communication of Thumping Waters

The King Who Learned Paperwork

If you had told me years ago that King Vesket—the enormous, scale-covered, spear-waving lizardfolk who once tried to feed us to a ghost—would end up as one of the Republic’s most competent mayors, I’d have laughed myself right into the river. And yet here we are. Life’s funny like that. Terrifying, but funny.

Back then, Vesket ruled the Longtail tribe from a muddy throne on an island fortress surrounded by wooden stakes and bad decisions. He was under the thrall of a will-o’-wisp, a cruel and clever thing pretending to be the spirit of his ancestors. It whispered lies into his ear—about destiny, vengeance, and glory—and Vesket believed them all. The Tubthumpers freed him from that glowing parasite’s influence, though it took a great deal of shouting, bravery, and one ill-advised lightning spell. When the smoke cleared, the wisp was gone, Vesket was blinking in confusion, and the rest of us were reconsidering our life choices.

What happened next still surprises me. Vesket did not walk away from his people, and the Longtails did not stop being Longtails. Instead, he somehow added Mayor to the collection of things everyone was expected to call him and discovered that governing a river town involves less throne-sitting and considerably more discussion of bridge maintenance. I remember him saying, “The river flows forward, not back.” I wrote that one down immediately. It’s still one of my favorite quotes from a seven-foot-tall reptile with better penmanship than Seamus.

When I visited Longtail Island again years later, the place had changed completely. The muddy huts were gone, replaced by paved streets, workshops, and a proper keep. The air smelled of sawdust and bread instead of swamp gas. Vesket greeted me at the town hall, wearing a cloak instead of a crown and holding a clipboard. He gave me a tour of the granary, the barracks, and even the sewer system, of which he is unreasonably proud.

That was when I finally understood what had changed. Vesket had not become less Longtail. He had simply decided that protecting his people meant more than winning fights. Sometimes it meant drainage. Sometimes it meant trade agreements. Sometimes it meant knowing exactly which office had failed to order replacement bridge bolts before the spring floods.

We used to fear the Longtails. Now we trade with them, celebrate with them, and cross their bridges without a second thought. Watching torchlight ripple across the Shrike that night, I realized that redemption is rarely one grand gesture. Usually it’s years of showing up, doing the work, and refusing to become the worst thing you once did.

—Linzi
