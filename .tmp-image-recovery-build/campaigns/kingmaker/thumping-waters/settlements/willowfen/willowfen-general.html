Willowfen General

A Lantern and Ledger guide to Willowfen’s principal store, compiled from the settlement gazetteer, caravan records, and the travel recollections of Velka of the Sootscale

We carry everything a new village needs, except dry ground. The mayor says that is coming in next week’s wagon.

— Mira Tolvane

Willowfen General is the general store, supply depot, message post, notice board, and rumor exchange at the center of Willowfen. In a village barely older than some of its green-pine walls, the store performs work that more established settlements divide among warehouses, markets, post offices, guild halls, and several varieties of clerk.

The business is owned and supplied by Mara Olegsdottir, whose connections to Olegton keep its wagons moving west. Its daily operation belongs to Mira Tolvane, the resident shopkeeper who knows what remains on every shelf, which household can spare a tool, and which apparent emergency may be solved with rope.

At a Glance

Type: General store, supply depot, message post, and meeting place

Location: Central Willowfen, opposite the Silver Reed

Owner and Provisioner: Mara Olegsdottir

Resident Manager: Mira Tolvane

Known For: Variable stock, honest credit, job notices, caravan news, and a driftwood counter

Primary Suppliers: Olegton caravans and local trappers, fishers, and craftspeople

Associated Person: Velka of the Sootscale

Local Description: The room where Willowfen finds out what Willowfen is doing

The Store at the Center

Willowfen lies roughly fifty miles west of Thumpington in a low, mist-filled basin of the northern Narlmarches. Its houses stand upon short stilts, plank walks cross the worst mud, and packed-earth trails are called streets through optimism and repetition. Willowfen General occupies one wide timber room near the village center, directly across from the Silver Reed and close to the new town hall.

The location makes the store a natural civic crossroads. Settlers visit for nails and lamp oil, then remain to read the notice board. Trappers bring hides and woodland news. Fishers compare water levels. Caravans unload supplies while Acting Mayor Tarin Veln negotiates for materials needed to keep the walls, walks, and public buildings upright.

People commonly say that Mira Tolvane runs Willowfen General. The legal and commercial truth is more precise: Mara Olegsdottir owns the business, arranges much of its caravan trade, and carries its larger financial risks, while Mira manages the counter, depot, accounts, and village relationships. Neither woman considers the distinction mysterious unless someone is trying to avoid paying one of them.

History

Willowfen was founded on 3 Desnas 4713 AR by loggers, trappers, fisherfolk, and other settlers pushing deeper into the western woods. Surveyors judged the ground mostly stable provided nobody jumped, and construction began before anyone had determined whether that statement was reassurance or prohibition.

The general store opened as a supply tent beside the first wagon track. Mara Olegsdottir recognized that the young settlement needed a reliable receiving point for shipments from Olegton, while Mira Tolvane understood that goods alone were insufficient. Someone had to record who needed them, who could pay, what had gone missing, and which sack marked seed grain must not be consumed during a rainy afternoon.

The tent gave way to the present timber building as Willowfen’s center took shape. Its walls, roof, and shelves have been altered repeatedly as the settlement grows. The structure is less a finished shop than a well-maintained answer to a question that changes every week.

The Building

The Driftwood Counter

The store’s broad counter was built by Mira and Velka of the Sootscale from driftwood hauled downriver and seasoned beneath cover. Velka called it blessed timber. Mira calls it a counter and oils it every month with the attention ordinarily reserved for an altar.

Its river-worn grain has become one of Willowfen’s first recognizable landmarks. Children trace the darker lines while adults wait to be served. A row of shallow marks records deliveries that arrived before the floor was level enough to keep inkpots upright.

The Shelves

Willowfen General’s stock changes with each caravan. A fortunate week may bring lantern oil, saw blades, nails, salt, boots, lamp wicks, rope, dry blankets, and medicines. Another may bring little beyond soap, turnips, mismatched hinges, and an entrepreneurial quantity of buttons.

Mira divides the shelves by use rather than prestige: light, cutting, fastening, food, clothing, medicine, fishing, trapping, and things someone will absolutely need after insisting they do not. Dangerous goods and scarce medicines remain behind the counter. Dry goods are raised well above the floor.

The Supply Depot

A reinforced rear room receives bulk deliveries and civic supplies. Timber, grain, salt, lamp oil, and construction hardware may be held there for distribution through the town hall. The depot contains a raised loading platform because wagons sink, floors flood, and experience is cheaper than replacing flour.

During severe weather the store tracks available rope, boats, dry rooms, preserved food, and people capable of repairing roofs. The town hall remains Willowfen’s formal refuge and meeting place; the general store is often where residents learn what to bring there.

The Notice Wall

One interior wall serves as Willowfen’s public information exchange. Notices are grouped beneath painted symbols for work, trade, travel, missing property, civic needs, and warnings. Mira reads notices aloud for anyone who requests it and will rewrite an unclear message before allowing it to create three days of unnecessary panic.

The message cubbies beneath the board receive letters and parcels carried by wagon or traveler. Delivery within Willowfen is informal: recipients collect messages themselves or someone carries them along when heading in the right direction.

Mara Olegsdottir

Mara Olegsdottir is the owner, principal buyer, and caravan organizer behind Willowfen General. Her business relationships in Olegton allow the store to combine household orders, civic requests, and speculative goods into shipments worth sending across an unfinished road.

She visits Willowfen with caravans and spends much of her time negotiating freight, inspecting losses, and deciding which scarce goods should be ordered next. Mara accepts that her stock may vary wildly. She does not accept vague inventories, mysteriously lighter barrels, or the claim that a wagon containing only soap and turnips represents a balanced emergency shipment.

Mira Tolvane

Mira Tolvane manages the shop and has become one of Willowfen’s essential civic figures without holding public office. She is practical, observant, and skilled at distinguishing a settler who cannot pay from one who merely hopes not to.

Her strength is local knowledge. Mira knows which household has spare nails, which carpenter can repair a broken axle, which trapper is overdue, and which newcomer arrived with a useful skill but no idea whom to tell. She exchanges this knowledge carefully; the store is a rumor hub, but she refuses to turn private hardship into public entertainment.

Mira leaves a bowl of clean water beside the door each morning. She says it keeps dust down, an explanation treated with great solemnity in a settlement where dust is largely theoretical. She always glances toward the window where Velka sang the store’s first blessing.

Trade and Credit

The store accepts coin, local goods, labor vouchers authorized by the town hall, and short-term credit. Mira records every debt but does not display the ledger. Interest is modest on ordinary household necessities and higher on speculative purchases, luxury goods, or items ordered after the buyer was specifically warned against them.

Local fish, hides, herbs, simple woodwork, and other usable goods may be traded against store purchases. Mira will not buy more perishable food than the settlement can consume, regardless of how heroic the gathering story sounds.

A small emergency reserve is maintained separately from commercial stock. Acting Mayor Tarin Veln may request its release for fire, flood, structural failure, or missing travelers, but every item is recorded afterward. Willowfen’s government may be provisional; its nails are not.

Velka at Willowfen General

Velka helped Mira build the driftwood counter and sang the first blessing performed within the completed store. She visits when her travels carry her through Willowfen, bringing small river-found objects, news from other settlements, and an instinctive willingness to perform whatever work is currently delaying supper.

Mira keeps Velka fed and informed. Velka helps keep the store dry, calms frightened newcomers, and tells children stories while they race driftwood boats through the drainage ditches. Their friendship is unceremonious: neither needs to announce that the other is welcome.

The bowl beside the door has become a quiet Hanspurite custom, though the general store is not a shrine. Travelers sometimes touch the water before departing west. Others use it to clean mud from their hands. Velka considers both uses entirely appropriate.

Customs of the Store

The First Open Crate

When a caravan arrives, the first crate is opened in public unless it contains medicine, private goods, or something likely to bite. The practice reassures residents that supplies have reached Willowfen and prevents optimistic rumors from improving the contents before inventory begins.

The Spare Plank

A sound board is kept beneath the front awning for emergency repairs to the nearest walk. Anyone may use it, provided they return an equally useful plank when able. The custom has saved more ankles than several official construction plans.

No Mocking the Mud

Willowfen residents tolerate observations about weather, insects, fog, unfinished streets, and damp socks. A visitor who mocks the settlement’s mud while demanding its hospitality may discover that every item in the shop is technically available but regrettably stored upon the highest shelf.

Place in Willowfen

Willowfen General is the practical heart of a settlement that has not yet had time to grow separate institutions for every need. It supplies loggers, trappers, fishers, builders, travelers, and the provisional government. It helps caravans turn cargo into construction and helps scattered residents behave like neighbors.

Across the central track, the Silver Reed provides beds, food, drink, and an evening hearth. Between the two establishments, a traveler can learn what lies ahead, purchase what the road requires, and reconsider leaving until the rain stops.

Willowfen’s walls may still smell of sap and its streets may still attempt to retain passing boots, but the store’s counter is worn smooth in places. For so young a village, that small evidence of repeated use is a kind of history.

A new settlement is mostly a list of things it hasn’t got yet. Our work is to shorten the list without letting anyone forget what has already arrived.

— Mara Olegsdottir
