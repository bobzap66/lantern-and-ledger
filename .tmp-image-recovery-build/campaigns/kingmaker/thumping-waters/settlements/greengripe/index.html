Greengripe

Compiled by the College of Letters and Public Record, Free University of Thumping Waters, with field assistance from Nok-Nok College of Practical Arts and Civic Service and the Citizen Council of Greengripe

Greengripe does not laugh because nothing frightens it. Greengripe laughs because fear has never deserved the final word.

— Linzi

Greengripe is a predominantly goblin town along the western trade road of the Republic of Thumping Waters. Once a ramshackle hilltop settlement mistrusted by its neighbors and manipulated by a hidden cult, it grew under Governor Nok-Nok into one of the Republic’s most prosperous and distinctive communities. Its breweries, alchemical laboratories, workshops, markets, and testing grounds make it a center of goblin industry whose products travel far beyond the Greenbelt.

The town is equal parts workshop, brewery, civic experiment, and festival. Its streets smell of hops, forge smoke, forest rain, and alchemical reagents whose exact composition may be protected by trade secrecy or public safety law. Greengripe’s appearance suggests joyful disorder. Its continued prosperity demonstrates how carefully much of that disorder is maintained.

Greengripe entered 4716 AR without the governor who shaped its modern identity. Nok-Nok and most of his Irregulars died during the War of the River Kings. The settlement has answered that loss by preserving what he built rather than pretending another leader can become him.

The Town among the Hills

Greengripe stands near the western trade road amid dense forest and rolling hills. Wooden walls surround the settlement, their surfaces protected by coats of paint whose colors rarely match from one section to the next. A stone watchtower rises above the main gate beneath shifting displays of banners, warning flags, civic decorations, and experiments whose official classification depends upon whom one asks.

Paved streets wind among crooked but sturdy buildings of timber and stone. The structures reflect goblin preferences for adaptation, reuse, dramatic color, and adding one more useful room after an architect has declared the building complete. Rooftops support vents, rain collectors, drying racks, small gardens, mechanical devices, and occasionally citizens who insist they are conducting work.

The center of town contains the market, town hall, principal workshops, and public park. Residential streets spread outward toward the walls, while more dangerous alchemical and demolition work is increasingly directed toward prepared grounds beyond crowded neighborhoods. The distinction between dangerous and interesting remains culturally important and administratively exhausting.

Greengripe’s sewer system is a point of local pride. It keeps the streets cleaner than visitors expecting a goblin camp often anticipate and supports the breweries and workshops whose waste would otherwise enter nearby soil and water. Maintenance crews possess broad authority to halt any experiment likely to damage the system, a power they exercise with less humor than most offices in town.

Before the Republic

Greengripe’s first sustained contact with the Tubthumpers came during an investigation into a Bloom affecting the Republic. Evidence suggested that goblin cultists in the Sellen Hills might be responsible and led the company to Greengripe on 30 Arodus.

The settlement was then led by a figure calling himself Chief Muglund. When the visitors requested an audience, Muglund attacked and used the resulting confusion to turn the settlement against them. The Tubthumpers suspected that the people around them might be victims or pawns rather than willing participants in the cult. They fought their way out without attempting to slaughter the town and carried the chief away for questioning.

Their restraint did not prevent tragedy. During the confusion, Wapati, Fitzroy’s elk companion, trampled and killed a goblin civilian. The death was accidental. It was also real, and good intentions did not remove the responsibility it created between Greengripe and the outsiders who had entered it.

Muglund was soon exposed as Virthad, a doppelganger priest of Lamashtu who had murdered Greengripe’s true spiritual leader and taken his place. Virthad intended to make goblins appear responsible for the Bloom while the cult’s real work continued elsewhere. The discovery proved that Greengripe had been manipulated. It did not require the settlement to forget the civilian killed during the investigation.

A later diplomatic mission acknowledged the harm, repaired relations, and negotiated Greengripe’s entry into the Republic. The town pledged fealty while retaining its own community and customs. Barracks, roads, public works, and national trade followed. Greengripe’s incorporation became an early test of whether Thumping Waters could distinguish membership from cultural surrender.

The Governorship of Nok-Nok

Nok-Nok transformed Greengripe. The goblin adventurer had called himself the Hero of the Mite Warrens since helping rescue Mikmek and recover the Sootscales’ stolen statue beneath The Old Sycamore. As governor, he turned the same defiant confidence toward administration.

He governed loudly, theatrically, and with considerably more attention than his public antics suggested. Nok-Nok visited workshops, knew citizens by name, encouraged inventors, listened to brewers and smiths, and took delight in the projects of local children. His speeches mixed civic information with comedy, boasts, demonstrations, and events later classified as unrelated accidents.

Nok-Nok referred to himself as the Chief of Fun and Fire Safety. His claim to the first office was broadly accepted. His qualifications for the second remained disputed. The standing order posted upon the town hall door became the most famous law in Greengripe:

NO EXPLOSIONS BEFORE BREAKFAST

Enforcement was inconsistent during his lifetime and has not improved since his death.

The governor’s real achievement was not making Greengripe orderly by the standards of another town. He helped its people turn their own habits, knowledge, humor, and ambitions into durable institutions. Goblins did not need to become less goblin to become citizens, officials, soldiers, merchants, or respected craftspeople. Greengripe remained noisy and unpredictable because its identity was protected, not because its government had failed.

Government after Nok-Nok

Greengripe’s governorship remains vacant following Nok-Nok’s death. Ordinary administration is conducted by the Citizen Council he worked beside: representatives drawn from the town’s tinkerers, brewers, smiths, merchants, public crews, and other established interests.

The council manages markets, roads, public safety, trade, workshop disputes, festivals, relations with the national ministries, and the continuing question of succession. It has declined to appoint an imitation Nok-Nok merely to fill the office quickly. Candidates for permanent governor face the difficult task of demonstrating that they can protect his legacy without treating every decision he made as sacred law.

The arrangement has proven more stable than outsiders expected. Nok-Nok’s personal influence had been enormous, but he had not governed alone. The council, clerks, crews, workshop leaders, guards, and ordinary citizens who operated Greengripe before his death continue to do so afterward.

Public meetings remain loud. Proposals are interrupted. Demonstrations occasionally occur before authorization. Decisions are nevertheless recorded, assigned, funded, and reviewed. Greengripe’s system does not resemble a quiet government. Quiet was never among its stated objectives.

Industry and Trade

Greengripe is one of the Republic’s principal centers of alchemical production. Major laboratories anchor an industry extending through independent workshops, suppliers, apprentices, herbalists, glassworkers, and merchants. The town produces medicines, tonics, reagents, smoke preparations, fire suppressants, explosives, and devices whose inventors insist that the correct category has not yet been recognized.

Brewing is both a major trade and a civic art. Local breweries supply ordinary ales, stronger festival drinks, specialty batches, and ingredients used by alchemists whose insistence that brewing and chemistry are entirely separate disciplines has never survived a tour of both facilities.

The smithy, stonemason, and lumberyard provide tools and material to Greengripe and the wider Republic. A herbalist and magic shop sell potions, charms, ingredients, and practical enchantments. The trade shop and central market bring local goods together with produce, tools, cloth, household wares, and imports traveling the western road.

Goblin inventors are known for adapting discarded material, combining established devices in unexpected ways, and building prototypes before anyone has completed the meeting intended to approve them. This method produces failures with unusual visibility. It also produces inexpensive tools, novel military equipment, improved brewing systems, mechanical toys, and solutions that more conventional workshops failed to consider.

Greengripe’s economic reputation has changed since its founding. Merchants once visited because goblin goods were cheap or curious. They now place contracts because the town can supply useful products in quantity. The distinction is a source of considerable local pride.

Fizzlesticks & Co.

Fizzlesticks & Co. is one of Greengripe’s best examples of experimentation becoming civic infrastructure. Founded by Griggit Fizzlesticks, the shop grew from a painted market cart into an alchemical laboratory, licensed producer, testing house, and teaching site for Nok-Nok College of Practical Arts and Civic Service.

Its work ranges from medicines, cleaning compounds, dyes, lamp oils, and fire suppressants to signal smoke, construction mixtures, water testing, and carefully controlled experimental products. The laboratory is designed around the assumption that failure can be useful provided it is contained, documented, and not allowed to become someone else’s emergency.

The building reflects that philosophy. Pressure-relieving roof panels, reinforced walls, copper ventilation, a bermed test yard, a wash garden, and strict warning systems make safety part of the workshop rather than an apology offered afterward. Its rule—If it explodes, record why. If it does not explode, record why not—captures Greengripe’s broader habit of treating mistakes as knowledge rather than shame.

Fizzlesticks & Co. also demonstrates how much Greengripe has changed since outsiders treated goblin invention as a curiosity. The shop fills civic and commercial contracts across the Republic, trains students and apprentices, and works closely with the town’s sanitation and fire crews. Its experiments may still be dramatic. The systems around them are not accidental.

The Laughing Bomb

The Laughing Bomb is Greengripe’s best-known tavern and one of the most fashionable public houses in the western Greenbelt. It serves travelers, merchants, local craftspeople, visiting soldiers, and residents whose workday has improved enough to celebrate or deteriorated enough to require company.

The tavern is known for strong drink, louder music, elaborate toasts, and seating positioned to provide a view of the public square. Its owners maintain that every scorch mark has historical value. Insurance assessors use a narrower definition of history.

Military celebrations once filled the tavern when the Irregulars returned from drill or campaign. Since the war, the establishment has also become a place where survivors, families, and former workers from the bombardier companies gather without requiring their meetings to become formal memorials.

Faith and the Shrine of Cayden Cailean

A shrine to Cayden Cailean provides one of Greengripe’s quieter public spaces. It is tended by Briska Aleheart, a cheerful priest who teaches that bravery, good humor, freedom, and shared drink can all become forms of prayer when practiced with responsibility toward others.

The Accidental God suits a community skeptical of solemn authority and proud of heroes who did not begin life as anyone’s model of respectability. The shrine offers blessings, counsel, aid to travelers, support for brewers, and a place to remember the dead without forbidding stories that make mourners laugh.

Other beliefs remain present in Greengripe, including older goblin traditions and private practices shaped by Lamashtu’s historic influence. Virthad’s deception left the town cautious toward anyone who claims exclusive spiritual authority while demanding secrecy, sacrifice, or enemies upon whom every misfortune can be blamed.

Culture and Defiant Optimism

Greengripe’s culture is built around invention, humor, noise, competition, and what Nok-Nok called defiant optimism. The phrase describes laughter used as armor against fear. It does not require pretending that danger, grief, or failure are unreal. It refuses to let them dictate every response.

Music spills from workshops and taverns. Enchanted streetlamps flicker in rhythm after sunset. The town hall serves as council chamber, public theater, command post, and venue for civic announcements whose entertainment value varies according to the speaker. Children race mechanical toys through the park and learn early which warning whistles mean watch, which mean move, and which mean find an adult who understands valves.

Festivals are frequent and heavily attended. Fireworks, brewing contests, music, public games, invention fairs, and competitive demonstrations occupy the town square and park. The fire brigade attends most celebrations as both emergency service and honored participant.

Visitors often describe Greengripe as chaotic. Residents usually accept the description while questioning its intended criticism. The town’s workshops open on schedule, contracts are filled, sewers function, streets are patrolled, taxes reach the Treasury, and public crews know precisely which kind of chaos they have been assigned to manage.

Nok-Nok’s Irregulars

Greengripe’s most famous military formation was Nok-Nok’s Irregulars, also known as the Greengripe Bombardiers. Nok-Nok recruited alchemists, brewers, tinkerers, scouts, and demolition experts into a company specializing in alchemical bombardment, smoke, confusion, and controlled destruction.

The Irregulars wore bright red sashes and iron helmets shaped like beer mugs. They marched to drums made from repurposed kegs and summarized their battle doctrine as dazzle, disorient, detonate. Their weapons included firebombs, smoke preparations, and alchemical surprises whose inconvenience was occasionally more important than their destructive power.

The formation earned national respect during the defense of Willowfen. Its bombardments helped break a Pitaxian invasion force containing mercenaries, mammoth cavalry, and wyverns before the enemy could secure the settlement. High Marshal Valerie and other officers who had questioned the unit’s methods acknowledged its discipline and battlefield value.

Later in the war, the Irregulars joined four other Republican armies against Kob Moleg’s mammoth cavalry east of Fort Drelev. The engagement became the Republic’s greatest military defeat. Four armies were destroyed as effective formations. Nok-Nok refused to abandon the Irregulars when their position collapsed and died buying time for soldiers who could still escape.

A small number of survivors returned. The company has not been reconstituted. Greengripe continues to possess alchemical experts, militia, veterans, and the knowledge required to create another bombardier force, but the Citizen Council has rejected the assumption that replacing a destroyed army is as simple as issuing its old equipment to new people.

Mourning the Governor

News of Nok-Nok’s death reached a town whose public identity had become inseparable from him. Greengripe honored its governor and the fallen Irregulars with fireworks. For one night, no one was expected to enforce the law against explosions before breakfast.

The memorial did not make the town quiet. Workshops closed long enough for families to gather. Taverns filled with stories. Fireworks rose over the walls in colors associated with the Irregulars, and the sounds continued until grief and celebration became impossible to separate.

Nok-Nok’s standing order remains upon the town hall door. His title of Chief of Fun and Fire Safety has not been reassigned. Greengripe remembers him through the institutions he created, the citizens he defended, the industries he encouraged, and the civic assumption that being underestimated is an opportunity rather than a verdict.

The Greengripe Amphitheatre

The Greengripe Amphitheatre stands in Riverbend Park in Thumpington rather than within the settlement whose name it bears. Dedicated during the Victory Festival of 6 Gozran, 4715 AR, it honors Nok-Nok and the fallen Irregulars.

The decision to build a performance space rather than a silent monument reflects Greengripe’s influence. Music, comedy, civic gatherings, university ceremonies, and festivals keep the memorial among the living. Delegations from Greengripe regularly participate in its events and maintain a vigorous interest in the quality, height, and timing of its fireworks.

May every cheer raised within the Greengripe Amphitheatre remind us that the freedom to celebrate was purchased by those willing to stand in its defense.

— Memorial dedication

Greengripe and the Free University

The Free University of Thumping Waters named one of its founding colleges for Nok-Nok. Nok-Nok College of Practical Arts and Civic Service teaches alchemy, field medicine, emergency response, wilderness survival, defensive construction, scouting, settlement administration, and other disciplines closely aligned with Greengripe’s practical knowledge.

The Nok-Nok Irregulars Fellowship supports students whose talent might be missed by conventional academic examinations. Greengripe nominates candidates from its workshops, public crews, military families, and apprenticeships. Preparatory instruction allows students without formal schooling to enter advanced study without treating lack of previous opportunity as lack of ability.

The relationship works in both directions. University students and instructors visit Greengripe for practical work in alchemy, brewing systems, sanitation, emergency planning, and adaptive construction. Greengripe craftspeople teach as visiting practitioners without being required to exchange demonstrated expertise for academic manners.

Some residents remain cautious about allowing a national institution in Thumpington to define Nok-Nok’s meaning. The university acknowledges that Greengripe is not merely a source of students, stories, or memorial symbols. It is the community he governed and the principal authority upon what his legacy looks like in daily life.

Greengripe in 4716 AR

Greengripe remains prosperous, inventive, and politically stable despite wartime losses and an unsettled succession. Its industries have resumed ordinary trade. Veterans and survivors have returned to workshops whose work did not pause simply because the people beside them were missing. The Citizen Council continues to govern while the town decides what qualities it wants in its next governor.

The central political question is not whether Greengripe can survive without Nok-Nok. It already has. The question is how a community built around an extraordinary leader converts personal loyalty into institutions without sanding away everything extraordinary about itself.

Visitors still enter beneath mismatched walls and bright banners. The air still smells of hops and smoke. Music still follows the streets after sunset, and the warning whistles remain worth learning. Greengripe has grieved without becoming a tomb.

When the smoke clears, Greengripe still stands.

From the Journal of Linzi, Minister of Communication of Thumping Waters

Boom, Brew, and Bravery

If you enter Greengripe, hold onto your hat and do not ask why the air smells like hops and brimstone unless you have time for the answer. Greengripe is not merely a town. It is an experience: loud, confident, industrious, and occasionally combustible.

Nok-Nok once met me at the gate wearing armor that may have begun its life as a kettle. He bowed, tripped, and announced that the fall was part of the ceremony. The crowd applauded. That was when I understood that Greengripe’s people do not laugh at chaos. They harmonize with it.

I watched goblins and humans work beside one another while brewers stirred vats, alchemists argued over dangerous liquids, and children chased mechanical toys whose degree of sentience remained disputed. Then the Irregulars marched into the square wearing red sashes and beer-mug helmets. Their demonstration illuminated the target, the forest, part of the road, and one extremely nervous chicken. The chicken survived and was later described as a veteran.

Afterward, we crowded into The Laughing Bomb. Nok-Nok called Greengripe’s courage defiant optimism. Watching those people toast one another with singed eyebrows and enormous smiles, I believed him. Greengripe does not merely build bombs. It builds the courage to laugh while the world is still smoking.

When I left, Nok-Nok gave me a small firework and told me to light it whenever I needed courage. I kept it upon my desk.

I still do.

—Linzi

Editor’s note: The Ministry of Defense once described Nok-Nok’s Irregulars as a disciplined and entirely safe formation. The definition of safe was doing a heroic amount of work in that sentence.
