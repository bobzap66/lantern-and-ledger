Sootscale Valley

Carved into the southern Greenbelt, Sootscale Valley is the principal kobold settlement of the Republic of Thumping Waters and the civic home of the Sootscale Kobolds. What began as a people fighting for survival in mines, warrens, and hostile territory has become a permanent community with its own government, industries, traditions, and place within the Republic.

The settlement’s identity is rooted in two histories at once. One belongs to the original Sootscale clan, whose alliance with the future Republic began during the struggle against Tartuk. The other belongs to the kobolds who later escaped Hargulka’s domination with Kereek and Prazil. Sootscale Valley grew from the meeting of those communities rather than from the erasure of either one.

From Clan Stronghold to Republican Settlement

The Sootscales first entered the Republic’s history as an isolated kobold clan manipulated by Tartuk, a false prophet whose schemes drove them into conflict with the mites beneath the Old Sycamore. The Tubthumpers’ intervention exposed Tartuk’s deception and restored Chief Sootscale’s authority, beginning an alliance that outlasted the crisis itself.

Years later, Kereek and Prazil came into the Republic’s orbit after escaping Hargulka’s control. Their knowledge helped the Tubthumpers strike at the Troll King’s forces, and the kobolds who survived that conflict were eventually brought into the protection of the Sootscale community. The resulting settlement became more than a refuge. It became a recognized part of Thumping Waters.

Chief Sootscale remained an important traditional leader during that transition. Kereek’s later rise added a more formal republican office to the community’s leadership, linking Sootscale Valley directly to the political structure of Thumping Waters. The change did not make the valley less kobold; it gave kobolds a stronger voice in the state growing around them.

Leadership

Governor Kereek now oversees Sootscale Valley’s civic affairs, trade, defense, and relationship with the wider Republic. His leadership is more calculating than ceremonial: he prefers negotiation, leverage, and preparation to open displays of force and has made the valley’s usefulness to its neighbors one of the foundations of its influence.

Chief Sootscale has retired from formal leadership but remains an important elder and source of continuity for the community. His experience carries particular weight in matters concerning clan history, diplomacy, and the dangerous early years before the Sootscales became citizens of the Republic.

Prazil remains closely associated with Kereek and the settlement’s defense. The two still present much as they always have: Kereek planning three moves ahead and Prazil prepared to solve whichever problem survives the planning.

The Valley Today

Sootscale Valley remains shaped by mining, stone, metalwork, and the practical cleverness of people accustomed to making useful things in difficult places. Workshops, homes, civic buildings, and older underground spaces sit beside newer streets and public works. The settlement has grown outward without losing the habits formed underground: careful storage, close cooperation, suspicion of wasted resources, and an instinct for making every chamber, tool, and scrap serve more than one purpose.

The valley’s economy still depends heavily on ore and worked material. Smiths, stonemasons, miners, haulers, and craftspeople supply both local needs and trade beyond the settlement. The community’s reputation for ingenuity has encouraged wider experimentation in metalwork, engineering, lighting, and other practical applications of magic, but the valley’s strength comes less from spectacle than from accumulated technical skill.

Festivals, council meetings, markets, work crews, and family warrens give the settlement a culture that is recognizably Sootscale rather than simply a smaller copy of a human town. Common and Draconic mingle in public spaces, older clan customs survive beside republican institutions, and younger kobolds have grown up regarding citizenship in Thumping Waters as part of their inheritance rather than an alliance negotiated by their elders.

Velka’s Riverside Shrine

One of the valley’s most distinctive landmarks is Velka’s Riverside Shrine, a Hanspurite place of worship and community landing on the settlement’s waterline. The shrine is deliberately modest: a carved basin, reeds, brass bells, driftwood posts, and a low landing used for prayer, rescue training, bathing, and launching small devotional rafts into the current. fileciteturn576file0L2-L2

Velka of the Sootscale founded the shrine, but she does not treat it as private property or a fixed temple. During major observances, worshippers build a temporary Current Shrine from driftwood, offerings, cords, shells, stones, and remembrances. The raft is then released downstream without a chosen destination. The permanent riverbank site is therefore less a place where Hanspur is kept than a place where worship begins and moves on. fileciteturn576file0L2-L2

The shrine also serves practical purposes. Rescue ropes, blankets, poles, lamps, and flotation gourds are kept nearby; children learn to swim there; and travelers exchange information about currents, weather, damaged crossings, bandits, and safe camps. Its place in the valley reflects Velka’s own teaching that reverence for water is inseparable from knowing how to survive it and helping others do the same. fileciteturn576file0L2-L2

Several local figures help maintain that tradition. Kikki the Reedwhisper cares for the shrine while Velka travels and records water levels, healing lore, prayers, and parables. Mek-Mek the Copperhand forged its brass bells and supplies metalwork for devotional charms. Old Mother Thruppa brings children to the riverbank and ensures that cold-water rites are followed by food whether theology requires it or not. fileciteturn576file0L2-L2

Faith, Memory, and Suspicion of Prophets

The Sootscales’ history with Tartuk left the community deeply suspicious of religious authority that demands obedience without proof. That history makes Velka’s role unusual and important. She is respected not because she claims supernatural status over the tribe, but because her faith is expressed through rescue, travel, work, and service rather than control.

Her shrine also openly rejects the crueler traditions sometimes associated with Hanspur. No unwilling victim is sacrificed there. Rites that confront drowning are voluntary and supervised, turning an old story of betrayal into a communal promise that no one entering the water will be abandoned. fileciteturn576file0L2-L2

That distinction fits the valley’s broader political culture. Sootscale Valley has already survived one false prophet and several would-be masters. Its people are unlikely to surrender judgment merely because someone arrives with a holy symbol, official title, or impressive speech.

Place in the Republic

Sootscale Valley is one of the clearest examples of the Republic’s plural character. The Sootscale Kobolds did not cease being a clan when they became citizens, and the settlement did not become important by imitating Thumpington. Its value lies precisely in the fact that it brought a distinct people, history, technical tradition, and political voice into the Republic without requiring them to disappear first.

For neighboring settlements, the valley is a source of ore, stonework, tools, trade goods, skilled labor, and practical expertise. For the Sootscales themselves, it is proof that survival need not mean hiding forever. The old instinct to retreat into tunnels has not vanished, but it now exists beside gates, roads, offices, shrines, and caravans carrying Sootscale work across Thumping Waters.

From the Journal of Linzi, Minister of Communication of Thumping Waters

The Spies Who Came in from the Swamp

When we first met Kereek and Prazil, they weren’t governors or heroes or anything close to respectable. They were spies. Bad ones. We caught them skulking around the streets of Thumping Waters, whispering to each other in Draconic and pretending to be very small bushes. Prazil sneezed and gave them away. Kereek immediately blamed him. It was, in hindsight, a very accurate preview of their relationship.

Kereek was all swagger and scales—bright red, sharp-eyed, and dressed like a festival banner someone had set on fire. He talked faster than most people think, and every sentence sounded like a plan. Prazil, on the other hand, was… simpler. A green-scaled mountain with a club, a grin, and the kind of loyalty that makes you forgive the occasional property damage. They were supposed to be working for Hargulka, the self-proclaimed Troll King, but the truth was much sadder. Their tribe was being held hostage, and spying was the only way to keep them alive. That, I think, was the first time I realized that not every villain’s shadow hides evil—sometimes it hides fear.

Once they confessed, Kereek started making suggestions—schemes, really. Big ones. Dangerous ones. The kind that start with “technically this isn’t treason” and end with fire. But he was clever, and more importantly, right. His knowledge of Hargulka’s forces let the Tubthumpers sneak into the troll’s fortress and end the war before it could spread. I still remember Kereek’s grin afterward, as if saving the kingdom was something he’d planned all along. Maybe it was. Or maybe he just didn’t like being on the losing side.

After the battle, the two kobolds were invited to stay. Most would have slunk away, but Kereek saw opportunity—and Prazil saw lunch. Together, they led their kin to the safety of Sootscale Valley, where clever hands and strong backs were finally welcome. Chief Sootscale saw potential in them both, though it was Kereek who caught his eye. The “clever tongue,” the “red-scaled schemer”—he had a spark the chief said belonged in a forge, not a dungeon. And for once, Kereek agreed.

Now, years later, I’ve watched that same kobold—once a liar and a spy—stand before his people as Governor Kereek. His voice carries confidence, his plans carry the republic, and his robe still looks like a bonfire in progress. Prazil stands beside him, smiling wide and proud, ready to smash anyone who threatens his friend. It’s strange to think that two misfits from a swamp changed the course of a kingdom. But that’s the beauty of Thumping Waters: we don’t throw away our rogues. We give them titles, jobs, and hopefully a few safety inspections.

—Linzi

Editor’s note: The Ministry of Communication wishes to clarify that espionage, while occasionally forgiven, is not an officially recognized path to public office.
