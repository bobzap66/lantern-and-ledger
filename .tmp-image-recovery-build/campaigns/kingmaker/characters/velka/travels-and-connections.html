Velka of the Sootscale – Travels and Connections

Though she calls the river home, Velka of the Sootscale has left her footprints across the Republic of Thumping Waters. From the markets of Thumpington to the youngest planks of Willowfen, she moves like water—binding the kingdom not with law or coin, but with kindness, faith, and the shared rhythm of work and mercy.

Velka in Embeth Hall

Nestled deep in the Embeth Forest, Embeth Hall is a strange blend of refinement and frontier—luxury built on the edge of the wild. The White Lantern tavern glows at its heart like a beacon through the mist, and for Velka, it’s a place that feels halfway between a shrine and a hearth.

Marna Drell – Proprietor of The White Lantern

Marna Drell runs The White Lantern, Embeth Hall’s famed tavern, with a warm smile and a sharper wit. Velka first wandered in drenched from the rain, following the lantern’s glow through the storm. Marna handed her a towel, a mug of Lanternlight Gin, and a fried pike sandwich without asking a single question. That simple kindness became the root of a lasting friendship.

Velka visits often, trading river stories for drinks and bringing Marna bits of driftwood to burn in the hearth for luck. The tavern now features her own creation—Hanspur’s Glow, a cocktail made from Lanternlight Gin and blue liqueur that faintly shimmers like moonlit water. When Velka’s in town, she blesses the dockworkers and fishermen before their evening rounds, and The White Lantern fills with laughter and river songs late into the night.

Marna calls her “my favorite flood hazard,” and Velka calls Marna “a light that even the river envies.” Between them, Embeth Hall feels like the heart of Hanspur’s mercy—where warmth meets water and faith tastes faintly of gin.

Velka in Fort Serenko

High in the Rostland Hinterlands, Fort Serenko stands as both bulwark and crossroads, its walls scented with pine and sawdust. The hum of saws from the lumberyard mixes with the sound of hammer on nail, the fort’s rhythm as constant as its river-fed millrace. Velka visits regularly, drawn by both friendship and the promise of honest labor.

Harl Edvane – Master of the Serenko Lumberworks

Harl Edvane, the grizzled foreman of Serenko Lumberworks, is built like a log and twice as solid. His beard is streaked with gray and sawdust, his voice like gravel ground in resin. When Velka first arrived looking for driftwood, he mistook her for a scavenger and nearly turned her away. But her kindness—and the quiet way she blessed his water troughs—softened his suspicion.

Over time, they’ve become close friends. Harl saves her the best scraps of river-worn wood for her talismans, and she brings him bottles of Lanternlight Gin in return. The two share long, companionable silences by the sawpits, speaking only when needed. He pretends not to believe in gods, but he keeps one of Velka’s charms hanging above his office door “for balance.”

When the river runs high and the logs threaten to jam, Velka walks the banks barefoot, murmuring Hanspur’s blessings, and the water always seems to loosen. Harl calls it luck. She calls it listening. Either way, the fort keeps standing.

Velka in Fort Tuskwater

Fort Tuskwater sprawls along the lake’s edge, half fortress, half fish market, its air thick with brine and smoke. The waterfront is alive at all hours—fishermen shouting, gulls crying, and traders arguing over eels. Velka is a welcome sight there, her scales glinting with spray as she blesses nets and jokes with the dockhands.

Marrek Holt – Proprietor of The Briny Wake

Marrek Holt, a broad-shouldered half-orc with laugh lines deep enough to hide secrets, runs The Briny Wake, a waterfront smokehouse and fishmonger. Velka met him during a bad season when the lake’s fish turned sickly. While others blamed bad bait, she waded out at dawn, whispering Hanspur’s rites of renewal. By dusk, the catches were clean again. Marrek’s been her friend ever since.

He keeps a crate labeled “Velka’s Bits” behind the counter—driftwood, shells, and scrap rope for her talisman work—and hangs one of her charms above his door. Velka often helps clean nets or bless shipments, saying, “If the fish swim willing, the gods are satisfied.” Marrek calls her “Captain Mudfoot,” a title she pretends to hate but secretly loves.

On calm nights, the two share bread and lake wine on the dock, watching the water shift beneath the stars. He says she brings peace to the shore; she says he brings laughter to the tide. Between them, Fort Tuskwater remembers that prosperity and faith flow from the same source: steady hands and a clean current.

Velka in Tatzlford

Tatzlford hums with the rhythm of work—the clang of hammers, the rasp of saws, and the sharp tang of resin in the air. The Guildhall of the Joined Hands stands at the center of it all, a meeting place for carpenters, smiths, and tanners who turn forest and ore into civilization. Velka fits right in. To her, every forge and workshop is a kind of chapel—proof that creation is just another form of prayer.

Garrin Whetforge – Blacksmith

Old Garrin Whetforge is a thick-browed blacksmith with more scars than patience. He swore off superstition years ago, but when Velka blessed his forge before a delivery run, his tongs stopped snapping and his temper cooled. Since then, he keeps a little driftwood charm above the anvil, muttering that “it keeps the sparks in line.” He’d never admit belief, but he never starts the day’s first hammer stroke until Velka’s finished her tea.

Lissa Tarmel – Carpenter

Lissa Tarmel, one of the Guildhall’s finest woodworkers, trades shavings and offcuts with Velka for use in her talismans. Together they built a tiny riverside shrine—little more than a post and a bell—where guild apprentices leave small carvings in thanks for steady hands and safe work. The bell’s tone changes with the wind, and Velka insists that’s Hanspur answering.

Fenno Pike – Leatherworker

Fenno Pike is a soft-spoken craftsman with an anxious streak. He swears the guild runs smoother when Velka’s in town. She teases him endlessly, but always blesses his glue pots and hides before she leaves. When she’s gone, he hums her hymns to keep the peace among the louder artisans.

To the people of Tatzlford, Velka is welcome friend. When she departs, the bell by the river rings until her boat disappears downstream, its echo a benediction on the busy little town.

Velka in Varnhold

Varnhold sits where the mountains meet the plains, its alehouses louder than its churches and its winters longer than patience itself. Yet even here, Velka finds a home. She claims the wind smells of “stone and second chances.” Her favorite haunt is Delver’s Brewery, where the air is thick with malt, laughter, and the faint hum of devotion disguised as fermentation.

Durnan Stoutbeard – Brewmaster of Delver’s Brewery

Durnan Stoutbeard, owner of Delver’s Brewery, is a barrel-shaped dwarf with a gravel laugh and a brewer’s pride in his signature stout—the Cheerful Delver. When the Crooked River flooded three years ago, he nearly lost a cellar of barrels until Velka waded in and whispered a prayer to Hanspur. The waters receded within the hour, and he’s sworn by her ever since.

Velka helps bless the water before each brew, and Durnan pays her in good ale and better company. Together they’ve perfected a ritual: she blesses the kegs, he drinks the first toast “for the god who keeps the taps full.” He keeps one of her braided hop charms above the brewroom door, and claims that every batch since has tasted like luck itself.

When Velka leaves, Durnan raises a mug to the current and mutters, “To the river’s favorite priestess.” The regulars echo it, and somewhere downstream, the foam gleams a little brighter.

Velka in Greengripe

There’s nowhere in the Republic quite like Greengripe: a riot of color, smoke, and explosive optimism. Goblins and alchemists work side by side—or sometimes against each other—in a constant attempt to improve on yesterday’s chaos. Velka calls it “Hanspur’s laughter made loud.” The locals call her River Auntie.

Griggit Fizzlesticks – Alchemist

Griggit Fizzlesticks runs Fizzlesticks & Co., a laboratory that smells perpetually of smoke, citrus, and disaster. Velka helps him gather swamp herbs and keep his experiments from catching fire—or from escaping. Together they invented Hanspur’s Breath, a bubbling tonic that cures hangovers, cleans wounds, and allegedly makes the drinker “more interesting.” Griggit insists her prayers make his potions “explode happier.”

Kuppa and Snip – Glassblowers of Gleams and Shards

Kuppa and Snip, a pair of married goblin glassblowers, run Gleams and Shards, a workshop filled with color and clatter. Velka brings them smooth stones and river sand from her travels; they melt them into swirling blues and greens for her talismans. When she departs, their shop windows glow faintly with watery light—a trick of the glass, or so they claim.

Professor Sparkleboom – Mage and Shopkeeper

Professor Sparkleboom, proprietor of Sparkleboom’s Curiosities, is an aging goblin wizard who argues with Velka almost daily. He calls magic “the science of will,” while she calls it “the patience of water.” They’ve reached a truce: she blesses his potions, and he enchanted her driftwood shield to glow blue when submerged—“for safety,” he says, though he smirks every time she uses it.

In Greengripe, Velka’s laughter is part of the town’s rhythm. Where others see chaos, she sees a current carrying invention forward. The goblins adore her—and when something inevitably explodes, she’s the first one they run to, not for scolding, but for praise.

Velka in Sootscale Valley

Sootscale Valley is Velka’s heart. It’s where the river first whispered her name, where she learned faith not from books but from survival. To her tribe, she is both priestess and sister—proof that devotion and freedom can share the same breath. Her shrine sits near the waterline: a carved stone basin surrounded by reeds, where travelers leave shells, coins, and shiny trinkets in thanks to the River Lord.

Chief Sootscale – Tribal Leader

Chief Sootscale trusts Velka more than most priests. She advises him on questions of faith, diplomacy, and restraint, reminding him that “even the strongest stream needs its banks.” He may grumble, but he listens—and more than once, her counsel has kept peace between the tribe and its neighbors.

Mek-Mek the Copperhand – Smith and Talisman-Maker

Mek-Mek the Copperhand, the tribe’s one-armed smith, grew up alongside Velka. He supplies her with scrap metal for her charms, and together they crafted the brass bells that hang over her shrine. When they chime, the valley’s children say it means Hanspur is listening.

Kikki the Reedwhisper – Herbalist Apprentice

Kikki the Reedwhisper is a young healer Velka mentors in the gentle overlap between herbcraft and faith. Together they gather river plants at dawn, Velka teaching that “clean water makes clean hearts.” Kikki records every prayer Velka utters, calling them “lessons in kindness disguised as stories.”

Old Mother Thruppa – Matriarch of the Nursery Warrens

Old Mother Thruppa, who claims she pulled Velka from the floodwaters as a hatchling, insists she’s proof that kobolds can be blessed and stubborn in equal measure. Every time Velka returns home, Thruppa makes her clam stew and demands to know why she hasn’t “built a temple like a proper priest.” Velka always laughs: “Because the river doesn’t sit still either.”

In Sootscale Valley, even the wind hums with her songs. The children race driftwood boats in the shallows; the miners hang her charms on their lanterns. When she leaves, the bells by the waterline ring until her shadow fades, a farewell from the tribe that raised her.

Velka in Olegton

Olegton sits where the road from Brevoy meets the road south toward Thumpington, a prairie crossroads surrounded by grassland and long horizons. Wagons creak through the old trading-post gates at nearly every hour, and the wind rarely seems to stop moving. There is no river here, which Velka finds both mildly offensive and deeply educational. “A place without a river,” she says, “teaches you what every bucket is worth.”

Velka’s visits tend to center on the town’s wells, cisterns, livestock troughs, and caravan water stores. She helps clean and bless them before the dry season, checks that travelers heading into the open country are carrying enough water, and has acquired a reputation for appearing beside an unattended rain barrel with the expression of a priest discovering a theological emergency.

Tavena Levet – Millkeeper and Cistern Keeper

Tavena Levet runs Levet’s Mill, whose sails turn in the prairie wind above Olegton, and helps oversee the cistern yard that serves nearby farms and passing caravans. She is loud, practical, and suspicious of anything that cannot be repaired with a wrench, rope, or sufficiently forceful language. She and Velka first became friends during a hard dry spell, when the town wells ran low and every stored barrel mattered.

Velka did not conjure a river or miraculously fill the wells. Instead, she spent days helping Tavena clean the cisterns, organize rationing, and bless each fresh delivery as it arrived. Tavena still insists that sensible planning saved the town. Velka agrees completely and calls that one of Hanspur’s better miracles.

A small bowl of clean water now sits near the mill door. Tavena says it is there to settle dust. Velka has never challenged her on this point.

Rurik Goldforge – Jeweler of Olegton

Rurik Goldforge, the dwarven jeweler of Goldforge’s Bench, met Velka when she came seeking fittings for her driftwood charms. He nearly turned her away for bringing prairie dust into the shop, but her habit of arriving with unusual stones, shells, and bits of polished glass gathered elsewhere in her travels eventually won him over.

Rurik turns those finds into small devotional pieces—a brass clasp etched with Hanspur’s ripples, a blue-glass pendant shaped like a falling drop, or fittings for one of Velka’s countless talismans. The pair often work side by side in silence while wagon bells and the groan of the windmill carry in from outside. Rurik calls her “my best unpaid supplier.” Velka calls him proof that even dwarves can understand that stone eventually has to listen to water.

Velka in Willowfen

Barely four months old, Willowfen still smells of new lumber and wet earth. The settlers joke that the mud has more permanent residents than the people, but Velka sees promise in its foundations. “Every settlement starts as a puddle,” she says. “This one just remembers where it came from.”

Mira Tolvane – Shopkeep of Willowfen General

Mira Tolvane runs the settlement’s humble general store—a single wide room that doubles as meeting hall, message post, and gossip hub. Velka helped her build its counter from driftwood hauled downriver, calling it “blessed timber.” Mira leaves a bowl of water near the door each morning “to keep the dust down,” though she always nods toward the window where Velka once stood singing the first blessing of the town.

Their friendship is simple but strong. Mira keeps Velka fed and informed; Velka keeps the store dry and the spirits high. Between them, the little shop hums like a heartbeat at the town’s center.

Sorn Delvik – Innkeeper of the Silver Reed

The Silver Reed Inn stands like a promise—a two-story beacon of light and laughter in the young town. Sorn Delvik, its half-elven owner, owes much to Velka. When his first well turned to mud, she blessed it with a prayer to Hanspur, and by dawn, the water ran clear. Since then, she’s been his unofficial partner in fortune: she helps cook, clean, and sometimes tell stories to the guests by firelight.

Children follow her everywhere when she visits, racing her driftwood boats through the muddy ditches.

Velka in Longtail Island

Longtail Island rings with the sound of forges and waves. Its artisans work close to the tide, shaping metal and coral in equal measure. To Velka, it’s a holy place—“where the hammer and the surf keep the same rhythm.” She’s drawn back here often, both by friendship and by the island’s understanding that faith and craft share the same current.

Ssekesh – Master Smith of Tailforge Atelier

Ssekesh, the lizardfolk smith of Tailforge Atelier, is a craftsman of quiet pride and long patience. His forge is open to the sea breeze, powered by tide-driven bellows, and his scales gleam the color of deep slate. He first met Velka when she sought to repair her all-metal trident. Most smiths balked at the challenge; Ssekesh only said, “Metal is the river that forgot how to move. Let’s remind it.” Together they reforged it under her blessing, the sparks reflecting like starlight on water.

Since then, Velka has returned often. She blesses the forge with river water before his first strike, and he quenches each finished piece in it. Their friendship runs deep and wordless, built on shared reverence for transformation—his through flame, hers through faith. Islanders say that when she’s present, the forge burns blue and the sea grows calm. Ssekesh denies it, but Velka only smiles and says, “The river remembers who listens.”

Velka in Thumpington

Thumpington is the Republic’s heart—a city where parchment, politics, and prayer all jostle for elbow room. To Velka, it’s less a capital than a confluence: a hundred smaller streams of people and ideas meeting in one great current. She visits often, tending shrines along the Thorne River and reminding ministers that “even a city of stone still depends on the water beneath it.”

Linzi – Minister of Communication

Velka and Linzi share a bond built on laughter, philosophy, and the shared belief that stories can heal a kingdom. The halfling bard delights in retelling Velka’s river parables—usually with added dramatic flair—and Velka pretends to scowl every time. Together they co-authored a pamphlet titled The River and the Road: Conversations on Freedom, blending Linzi’s keen commentary with Velka’s quiet Reflections on Hanspur’s balance. Linzi calls her “the Republic’s muddy philosopher.” Velka calls Linzi “a song that learned to walk.”

When Velka stays in the capital, she often lodges at the Ministry guesthouse, joining Linzi for tea and editing sessions late into the night. Between them, words and faith flow freely, keeping Thumpington’s conscience clear as its waters.

Jubilost Narthropple – Minister of Trade

Where Linzi and Velka share warmth, Jubilost and Velka share argument. Their debates—faith against finance, river versus ledger—are small spectacles around the docks. Yet beneath the sparring lies mutual respect. She once accompanied him down the Little Sellen to map safe trade routes, proving that Hanspur’s patience could rival even his precision. He grumbled about “divine inefficiency,” but later quoted her in a speech on adaptive trade policy.

Jubilost has quietly funded repairs to several riverside shrines she oversees, disguising it as “infrastructure upkeep.” Velka teases that he’s “the gnome who prays by accident,” and he calls her “a current that refuses to stay charted.” Between them, Thumpington learns that order and freedom can share the same flow.

Brina Fairlight – Candlemaker of the South Quarter

In a city of ministers and merchants, Brina Fairlight’s candlehouse glows like a quiet star. The air smells of honey, wax, and faintly of fur—thanks to her elderly white poodle, Esmerelda, who rules the shop’s doorway with benevolent disdain. Velka met Brina during a storm, when Esmerelda’s new litter whimpered through thunder and rain. Velka soothed them with a river hymn; one pup grew still under her touch—the same one later adopted by Ant and Ally, sired by the great Connal himself.

Since that night, Velka and Brina have been fast friends. Together they craft Hanspur’s Lights—blue-and-white floating candles made to drift on the river during prayer festivals. Brina handles the waxwork, while Velka blesses each with a drop of sanctified water. Their crafts—fire and water, patience and flow—mirror each other perfectly.

During the Midsummer Festival, the pair stand together on the docks, sending Hanspur’s Lights downstream as the city gathers to watch. For a moment, even Thumpington holds its breath, and faith flickers across the water like starlight on the current.
