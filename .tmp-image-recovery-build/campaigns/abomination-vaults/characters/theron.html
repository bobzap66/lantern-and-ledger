Theron

Tengu Fighter

active

Theron was an adventurer who joined the exploration of the Abomination Vaults beneath Gauntlight and ultimately helped bring about the destruction of both the lighthouse and its creator, Belcorra Haruvex. Originally a logger in Otari, Theron’s life changed irrevocably during the expedition when he was killed and later returned to life through primal reincarnation. He emerged from the ritual in the body of a tengu, beginning a second life that would prove considerably stranger than his first.

Among his companions, Theron became the group’s physical anchor: the fighter who stood between danger and the people less equipped to survive it. Death made him more pragmatic rather than more cautious. He became willing to accept ugly solutions when he believed they protected the greater number of people, but that ruthlessness existed alongside a genuine attachment to Otari and a desire to see the town recover from the fear cast over it by Gauntlight.

Death and Reincarnation

Theron’s first life ended in the Abomination Vaults.

His death left enough of an impression that, long afterward, he could still identify the place where his severed head had once screamed from a table. His companions nevertheless managed to recover him, and a primal reincarnation ritual returned Theron to life in a new body. The man who awakened was now a tengu.

The transformation was more than cosmetic. Theron had to adjust to a body fundamentally different from the one in which he had been born, including wings that eventually allowed him to take to the air. What began as an alien circumstance gradually became simply part of him. In time, Theron not only mastered flight but took genuine pleasure in it.

His experience also gave him an unusually practical perspective on resurrection. Having personally endured death and the psychologically complicated experience of awakening in another form, Theron understood that restoring a dead companion was not necessarily the simple reversal of a tragedy. Returning someone to life could save them while still changing them permanently.

Changing Form

This earlier portrait shows Theron before his death. The tengu portrait above records the shield, armor, and flaming sword through which his companions could still recognize the fighter they had lost. The body changed; the stance did not.

The Party’s Anchor

Theron’s place in the group was straightforward: when something dangerous needed to be stopped from reaching everyone else, Theron stood in front of it.

That role repeatedly put him in situations sensible people would have avoided. He plunged into an underground lake and cut open a monster that had swallowed Talia. He fought dragons in the air, battled graveknights and golems at close quarters, endured attacks intended to drain the life from him, and on more than one occasion survived wounds that left very little margin between victory and another death.

Not every example was heroic. Theron’s preference for solving immediate problems through direct physical action could produce spectacular mistakes. After discovering dangerous green slime clinging to the ceiling of a cavern, he elected to fly directly at it and attack it with his sword. The slime promptly engulfed him, sent him crashing to the ground, and spread to August when the cleric attempted to help.

Another expedition ended with Theron paralyzed by venom from an adamantine-toothed creature. Fully conscious but completely unable to move, he had to endure being dragged back to Otari while Talia read romance novels aloud to him.

Such incidents did little to change his basic philosophy. Theron knew what he was good at, knew what his companions needed him to do, and generally regarded the danger involved as part of the job.

The Crimson Fulcrum Lens

The struggle against Belcorra eventually led the adventurers to the Fulcrum Lenses, artifacts connected to the power necessary to defeat her permanently. Theron volunteered to carry the Crimson Fulcrum Lens.

The decision came with a severe cost. The artifact filled its bearer with supernatural anger, leaving Theron trapped in a state of constant irritation and seething rage. He remained capable of directing that anger toward the party’s enemies, but the emotion was not truly his, and its influence increasingly colored his already straightforward approach to problems.

While carrying the lens, Theron fought some of the party’s most dangerous battles. He knocked a bog dragon from the sky, survived attacks by urdefhan assassins, and helped defeat the graveknight Caliddo alongside a massive clay golem. When Caliddo’s cursed armor threatened to regenerate its owner, Theron enthusiastically supported the party’s distinctly unconventional solution: placing the armor into a magical Bag of Weasels until the object was transformed into something considerably less dangerous.

The lens remained with Theron until the final confrontation with Belcorra. When the time came to place it into the Fulcrum Lattice, he surrendered it without hesitation. The rage had been a burden he accepted because the party needed someone to carry it. Once it had served its purpose, he had no desire to keep it.

Against Belcorra

Theron proved particularly effective whenever Belcorra herself confronted the party.

During one manifestation, he struck the ghost with such overwhelming force that she fled from the encounter. A later confrontation in the Darklands settlement of Yldaris was even more consequential. The party entered the battle already weakened, but Theron recovered quickly enough to place himself between Belcorra and his companions.

When Belcorra attempted to unleash a devastating phantasmal calamity, Theron’s training allowed him to interrupt the spell before it could be completed. The attack prevented what the party later realized might have killed nearly everyone present.

The victory nevertheless carried consequences. Their presence had exposed the people of Yldaris to Belcorra’s retaliation, and Queen Quara banished the adventurers from the settlement. Theron accepted the judgment. Whatever their intentions, innocent people had been endangered because of them.

With their refuge gone and Belcorra certain to return, Theron’s response was characteristically practical. There was nowhere left to retreat. They still needed the remaining Fulcrum Lens, and the mission had not changed.

Khurfel and the Last Lens

The final Fulcrum Lens was held by Khurfel, an urdefhan warlord responsible for the deaths of members of Queen Quara’s family.

Theron met Khurfel in one of the expedition’s most brutal battles. The warlord tore into him with sword and jaws while reinforcements and enemy healers threatened to overwhelm the party. Theron absorbed the punishment long enough for his companions to create an opening, then killed Khurfel and immediately turned his weapon on the next enemy.

Among the possessions recovered afterward was Khurfel’s Roka sword, which was owed to Queen Quara in partial payment for the blood of her family. Theron took responsibility for keeping the weapon safe until it could eventually be returned.

More importantly, Khurfel’s death gave the party the last component they needed to end the threat of Gauntlight.

The Final Descent

Before entering the deepest reaches of the Vaults, Theron and his companions retraced the dungeon they had spent so long exploring. They walked through old battlefields, traps, cells, and rooms where friends had nearly died. Theron treated the journey less as a heroic pilgrimage than as a soldier reviewing an old campaign.

He even collected a warning note from one of the doors and kept it for his scrapbook.

The deepest chambers subjected the party to trials associated with Nhimbaloth. Theron was confronted with memories of an easier life and then with visions of his greatest failures. The torment eventually offered him an escape if he would surrender something of himself.

Theron refused.

“Much has already been taken from me. You will have nothing else.”

The answer captured what the Abomination Vaults had made of him. Theron had already died there. He had lost his original body, endured reincarnation, carried an artifact that filled his mind with rage, and repeatedly watched his companions come within moments of death. There was little the dungeon could threaten him with that it had not already done.

His deepest fear was not death itself. It was helplessness: the possibility that all his strength, armor, and training might someday prove useless while the people he protected died behind him.

He continued anyway.

The Fall of Gauntlight

Theron remained at the front through the last guardians of Nhimbaloth’s sanctum, including the ancient entity known as Lady’s Whisper. When the Fulcrum Lattice was finally ready, he surrendered the Crimson Lens so that the party could complete the mechanism intended to destroy Belcorra.

The plan worked.

Nhimbaloth’s power tore through Belcorra Haruvex, and the ancient ghost who had haunted Otari for centuries was finally destroyed. With its master gone and its purpose broken, Gauntlight began collapsing around the adventurers.

Theron ran.

The survivors emerged into the night as the lighthouse came down behind them, level after level of the Abomination Vaults disappearing beneath the ruins. Otari was waiting for them with drinks rather than speeches.

For Theron, it was an unexpectedly appropriate ending. He had first come to Otari as a logger whose ambitions extended little further than earning a living and staying out of trouble. By the time Gauntlight fell, he had died, returned in another body, learned to fly, carried a fragment of an Outer God’s power, fought his way through horrors beneath the earth, and helped destroy the ghost responsible for centuries of suffering.

Afterward, there was a fishing shack with six made beds.

Theron took one and slept.
