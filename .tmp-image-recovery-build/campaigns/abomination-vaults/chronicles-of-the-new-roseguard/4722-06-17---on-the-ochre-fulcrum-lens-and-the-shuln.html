Chronicles of the New Roseguard

On the Ochre Fulcrum Lens and the Shuln

The twenty-sixth installment in Morlibint’s retrospective history of the New Roseguard.

By Morlibint, Special Contributor — Absalom Bureau

17 Sarenith 4722 AR

The New Roseguard’s first attempt to search the great underground lake had ended with Talia Nightclaw and Kiri Steelpaws being swallowed by separate aquatic predators, which persuaded the company to reconsider the merits of searching several unknown acres of deep water by sight. Resting in the boathouse afterward also gave them time to remember that they already possessed magic intended to locate a particular object. There was one complication: the Crimson Fulcrum Lens they already carried was sufficiently similar to the Ochre Lens to interfere with the search, so it was placed within an extradimensional container before they tried again.

The resulting indication led northwest and beneath the lake. A descent into the water established that the bottom fell away much farther than their lights could usefully reveal, but the direction of the sought object was more westward than downward. They therefore abandoned the prospect of blindly dredging the depths and instead followed the western shore, using the magical indication to reduce a very large problem to a merely inconvenient one.

About half an hour later they reached a small island and adjoining shoreline thick with pale green crystals. The formations climbed the cavern walls and hung from the ceiling, and when Talia broke off a sample the disturbed crystal began to vibrate. Others nearby answered at the same pitch, followed by formations farther away, until the resonance had spread along the shoreline and across the island. Talia could identify no magic in the crystals themselves, but she quite reasonably observed that anything living nearby had now been given ample notice of the Roseguard’s presence.

The creature that appeared shortly afterward was a cauthooj, a large flightless predator whose hunting cry is considerably more dangerous than its appearance suggests. Its warbling can overwhelm judgment and turn listeners violently against whatever happens to be nearest. Comet recognized the danger quickly enough to answer the creature with music of his own, and for a time his performance prevented its call from taking hold of the others.

Repeated exposure eventually proved harder to resist. Theron, Talia, and Kiri each fell under the song’s influence during the encounter, and the distinction between fighting the cauthooj and fighting one another became temporarily unreliable. Talia clawed at herself before recovering enough purpose to resume shooting at the bird; Kiri attacked Talia; Talia responded by striking Kiri and later Theron in an effort to interrupt further violence. Theron also turned upon one of his companions before shaking off the effect. A more exact accounting of every misplaced blow would add length without producing much additional understanding, which is a sentence my editors have been waiting some years to see me write.

The cauthooj eventually left the island to pursue the company along the shore, where the Roseguard killed it. Its nest contained two eggs of sufficient rarity that Talia thought they might be valuable to a naturalist, trainer, or other specialist. More important for the purpose that had brought them onto the lake, the company then detected a magical emanation beneath the nearby water and narrowed its location closely enough that recovery was straightforward.

Comet answered the hunting song before it became everyone’s command.

The object brought up from the lake matched Queen Quara’s description of the Ochre Fulcrum Lens. This supported her account that her father had thrown the artifact into the water rather than allow Khurfel to take it, although it did not independently establish every circumstance surrounding his death. With the Crimson Lens already in their possession, the Roseguard now held two of the three artifacts that Clathor and Basrade believed were connected both to Belcorra’s power and to the means of destroying her.

Quara’s inheritance returned from the lake.

Possession was not the same as comprehension, a distinction that became increasingly important as the expedition continued. The Roseguard had acquired the two lenses in rapid succession and had not yet spent enough time examining either to understand its properties fully. They knew that a Fulcrum Lens could open certain sealed passages in the lower Vaults and that the twins of Yldaris believed the three objects had to be brought together for some greater purpose. Much beyond that remained conjectural on 30 Neth, and their immediate decision was to continue mapping the western shore rather than experiment with artifacts they did not yet understand.

One descending passage proved so steep and prolonged that the company concluded it led away into the greater Darklands rather than toward anything immediately connected with Gauntlight. They marked it and returned. A narrower opening farther along the shore was almost sealed by thick fungal growth, but Theron pushed through and discovered a small spring of fresh water beyond it. Continuing through the passage brought the explorers to another cavern, where the tail and hindquarters of a dead lizard protruded from among the fungi.

Talia examined the carcass and determined that it had bled to death from substantial wounds rather than disease or ordinary decay. The injuries resembled those produced by one of the burrowing predators the company had encountered elsewhere, and she had scarcely finished describing the similarity when the floor and walls began to shake. A massive animal then tore directly through the northern wall and entered the cavern.

The creature was a shuln, a burrowing predator of the upper Darklands with the broad body and digging habits of a mole greatly enlarged beyond any reasonable agricultural comparison. More unusual is the shuln’s ability to concentrate adamantine within its teeth and claws, apparently through its own metabolism. This allows the animal to excavate stone with remarkable efficiency and also means that armor, however reassuring against conventional teeth, is confronted with much the same material used to cut the armor in the first place.

Theron was among the first to experience the other principal danger of the species. The shuln’s bite carried a potent paralytic venom, and after being wounded he progressively lost control of his limbs until he could no longer move. August managed briefly to counter some of the venom’s effects, but the poison remained in Theron’s body and soon imposed paralysis again. The distinction between suppressing a symptom and neutralizing its cause became inconveniently practical while a several-ton burrowing animal remained in the room.

Kiri stayed close to the creature and fared little better. One of the shuln’s bites closed across her scale armor with sufficient force that its adamantine teeth tore through the metal and destroyed the harness beyond useful repair. She continued fighting after losing its protection but was also poisoned, and her own movements became progressively slower as the venom took hold.

With Theron helpless and Kiri deteriorating, the rest of the Roseguard had to keep the creature occupied while trying to find ways through its unusually durable hide. Talia studied the shuln’s anatomy as the fight progressed and directed Kiri toward softer tissue between the tougher portions of its body. “You see that stretchy piece of skin right there?” she called. “It’s connected to his body. That’s weak. It’s thin. It’ll bleed.” The explanation would trouble an anatomist, but it conveyed the necessary information.

Late in the fight, magic also disrupted the shuln’s senses badly enough that several attacks which might otherwise have struck true went astray. The surviving accounts preserve the impairment more clearly than its source, and assigning it to a particular member of the company would create certainty where the record no longer supports it. What can be established is that the distortion materially reduced the danger while Kiri, Comet, Talia, and August continued wearing the creature down, and August ultimately struck the blow that killed it.

Its teeth contained the material made to defeat armor.

Killing the shuln did not immediately restore either of its poisoned victims. Kiri and Theron remained rigid and unable to walk, Kiri’s armor was ruined, and the company had exhausted much of what it could conveniently do for them in the cavern. Continuing deeper into the Hunting Grounds would have required the remaining explorers to carry and defend two helpless companions while meeting whatever else lived there, so the expedition turned back toward Yldaris.

The retreat was considerably more laborious than the outward journey. Kiri and Theron had to be carried through the narrow fungal passages, transported across the lake, and then hauled through the remaining caverns while the others took care not to attract another predator. The surviving account estimates roughly two hours before the company reached the protection of Yldaris, where Queen Quara’s hospitality gave them a secure place to wait for the venom to run its course.

Talia remained with the two patients and quickly discovered a secondary problem: she had exhausted the book she had previously used to occupy paralyzed companions, while much of the reading material readily available in Yldaris was written in Elven. She therefore asked August to begin teaching her the language. Their lesson covered the alphabet, several children’s songs, and a handful of words August admitted he might not remember perfectly, while Kiri and Theron were in no position to find alternative instruction.

When voluntary movement finally returned, the Roseguard had several reasons not to resume the expedition that day. Kiri required new armor before facing the dragon the caligni had directed them toward; the cauthooj eggs might provide the means to obtain it without consuming what remained of the company’s funds; and Moadenn’s preserved remains were still awaiting consultation with someone capable of asking the dead whether she wished to be reincarnated. The newly recovered Ochre Lens and the Crimson Lens also deserved study before either was carried into another unknown chamber.

The company therefore planned to return to Otari once Kiri and Theron were fit to travel. Their immediate business there was practical: replace Kiri’s destroyed equipment, find an appropriate use or buyer for the cauthooj eggs, determine Moadenn’s wishes, and learn more about the two Fulcrum Lenses before descending again.
