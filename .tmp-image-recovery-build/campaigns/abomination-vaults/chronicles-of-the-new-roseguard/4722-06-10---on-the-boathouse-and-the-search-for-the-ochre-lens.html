Chronicles of the New Roseguard

On the Boathouse and the Search for the Ochre Lens

The twenty-fifth installment in Morlibint’s retrospective history of the New Roseguard.

By Morlibint, Special Contributor — Absalom Bureau

10 Sarenith 4722 AR

The death of Moadenn left the New Roseguard with a question they had previously answered for one another largely by necessity. Her body could be preserved, and August Shepard knew a rite by which the dead might be returned through primal reincarnation, but none of them knew whether Moadenn would have consented to such a thing. They had known her as herself for only a few hours. Talia therefore argued that possibility was insufficient and that, before attempting anything, they should use appropriate funerary magic to determine what Moadenn herself wanted.

Theron supported the distinction from experience. His own return after Jafaki killed him had restored his life but given him an entirely different body, and he cautioned the others against treating reincarnation as though it were simply another form of healing. “There’s a lot that goes into that,” he told them. “It ain’t just the physical changes.” The discussion broadened from Moadenn to the company itself, producing the useful and somewhat overdue result that several members stated their wishes while still capable of doing so.

August wished to see the struggle against Gauntlight completed but otherwise preferred that his soul be allowed to continue its proper journey. Theron, despite being grateful for his own second life, did not want another reincarnation if he died again. Talia would accept restoration as herself, but not reincarnation into some unfamiliar body merely because the means were available. Kiri had no comparable objection and was willing to be reincarnated if circumstances required it. These were not theoretical questions in a company whose recent membership included people who had died, been reincarnated, become undead, acquired vampiric traits, or undergone involuntary fleshwarping; precision on the subject had practical value.

They preserved Moadenn’s remains and placed them in one of their extradimensional carrying bags until they could seek her wishes properly. The process was interrupted while they were still extracting her from beneath the dead puffball, when several ceustodaemons emerged among the towering luminous fungi. Such fiends are often employed as bound guardians and could move abruptly across the cavern by magical means, making their apparent positions unreliable. They also breathed fire. The fight was unpleasant but brief, and the Roseguard destroyed them all after concluding that an attempted survivor was unlikely to provide useful information about whoever had originally bound it.

The battle brought the company to the lower end of the enormous iron ladder they had seen descending from the fungal levels above. Nearby structures stood along the shore of the underground lake, one of which appeared from information supplied in Yldaris to belong to another group of caligni. This created a diplomatic difficulty. Dulac had banished the Roseguard from caligni territory the previous day, and the company had agreed to respect that prohibition, although they did not know whether every caligni community in the Hunting Grounds recognized Dulac’s authority.

They had already neutralized an outer magical ward before recognizing who occupied the building. Once they did, they stopped short of forcing the barred doors and knocked instead. The inhabitants answered in Sakvroth and were not especially welcoming; August’s translation of their first question was considerably more profane than “Who are you?” They nevertheless learned something useful. The caligni told them to travel east, kill a dragon troubling the region, and return afterward if they wished to talk. Requests for a more exact description of the dragon produced little beyond the information that it was powerful, dangerous, and somewhere to the east, qualifications broad enough to encompass a regrettable percentage of dragons.

The Roseguard decided not to force the issue. Their more immediate objective was the Ochre Fulcrum Lens, which Queen Quara’s father had thrown into an underground lake rather than allow the urdefhan warlord Khurfel to take it. The lake beside them corresponded to Quara’s account closely enough to justify a search, but before committing themselves to the water they continued examining the buildings along its shore.

One was a squat stone boathouse, and here the evidence was unusually clear before anyone opened anything. Its entrances had been barricaded deliberately from the outside with old planks. The Roseguard’s previous experience with barricaded doors beneath Gauntlight included both imprisoned allies and imprisoned things whose confinement had been an excellent idea, so they first listened and then knocked. A voice answered in Common.

The speaker called himself Beluthus. He claimed that urdefhans had imprisoned him and said that he was starving. When asked whether he would harm the company if released, he assured them that he would not. The age of the barricades suggested he had been confined for a considerable period, which did not resolve the question of whether opening the door was wise but apparently did make the question more interesting.

The boards were removed. Beluthus immediately lunged through the doorway at Theron, reaching toward him with necromantic energy and announcing, “I’m coming, mommy.” Whatever confidence the company had placed in his assurances expired somewhat earlier than Beluthus did.

They identified him during the ensuing fight as a devourer, an especially dangerous form of undead arising from powerful and corrupted souls. The name is descriptive rather than ornamental: such creatures consume the souls of the living and use what they take to sustain their own magic, with consequences considerably more difficult to remedy than ordinary death. The Roseguard therefore had good reason to prevent Beluthus from reaching anyone long enough to feed. They did so, overwhelming him inside the confined boathouse before he could consume a soul.

His promise survived until the door opened.

The purpose of the old barricade was thus established more satisfactorily than its history. Beluthus’s claim that urdefhans had imprisoned him may have been true despite his lie concerning what he intended to do upon release, but the company obtained no further testimony from him and found no surviving record in the building that established who had sealed it. What the boathouse did contain was more immediately useful: a serviceable rowboat suspended from the rafters and a sea chest holding several valuable enchanted objects, including an armor rune, a magical dagger, and a feather token capable of producing a much larger vessel when needed.

The rowboat gave them a practical means of beginning their search for the Ochre Lens. August extended a blessing that allowed several companions to breathe beneath the water for many hours, while Talia relied upon an enchanted bottle that supplied her with air. The plan was reasonably systematic: keep part of the company with the boat as a place to regroup while stronger swimmers searched beneath and around it. Theron entered the lake first.

Almost immediately, something came up from below to investigate him. The creatures were deepwater dhuthorexes, large aquatic predators whose principal virtues, from their own perspective, were appetite and persistence. August’s knowledge of such creatures supplied one warning of particular importance: they were capable of swallowing prey whole and digesting it with alarming speed. He passed this information to those already in the water before the information had an opportunity to become personally relevant.

It became personally relevant quite soon. One dhuthorex seized Talia and swallowed her before diving deeper into the lake. Talia could still breathe, but breathing was only one of several problems associated with being carried downward inside a carnivorous animal. Her larger crossbow was useless in such cramped conditions, and she had to abandon it in favor of a small war razor while the others pursued the creature from outside. The dhuthorex was killed before it could carry her beyond reach, and Theron and the other swimmers cut through its body sufficiently to free her as the carcass continued to sink.

The second dhuthorex swallowed Kiri. Her situation was worse, since the animal turned away from the company and descended with her while much of the immediate rescue effort remained concentrated on Talia. Kiri possessed no knife small enough to use effectively inside the creature and was forced instead to struggle physically against it while its digestive fluids began doing what digestive fluids generally do, only faster.

By the time the others understood how far the second creature had carried her, Kiri was in serious danger and largely beyond their ability to assist quickly. She eventually forced her own way out and, rather than allowing the wounded dhuthorex another attempt to seize her, killed it before returning toward the surface. Her later complaint that the company had rescued Talia while leaving Kiri to rescue herself was not an entirely unfair description of events, although darkness, depth, and the simultaneous nature of the two attacks explain more of the disparity than Kiri was inclined to credit immediately afterward.

One rescue was shared; the other belonged to Kiri.

The experience produced one simple change in equipment. August gave Kiri the enchanted dagger recovered from the boathouse, and the company agreed that entering the lower Vaults without some small blade suitable for use in confined spaces had become needlessly hazardous. This conclusion had now been reached through more than one encounter involving the interior of a large animal, which is a sufficient evidentiary basis even by my standards.

Once everyone had returned to shore, the company treated its injuries and reorganized its equipment. The Ochre Fulcrum Lens had not been found, the eastern dragon remained where the caligni had indicated, and Moadenn’s preserved body still awaited a determination of her wishes. The Roseguard intended to return to the lake and continue the search.
