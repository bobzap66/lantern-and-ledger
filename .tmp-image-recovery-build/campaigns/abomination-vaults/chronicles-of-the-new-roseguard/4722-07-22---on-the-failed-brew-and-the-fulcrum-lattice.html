Chronicles of the New Roseguard

On the Failed Brew and the Fulcrum Lattice

The thirty-first installment in Morlibint’s retrospective history of the New Roseguard.

By Morlibint, Special Contributor — Absalom Bureau

22 Erastus 4722 AR

By the morning of 9 Kuthona, the New Roseguard had acquired several problems that could reasonably have justified a day of caution. Caliddo Haruvex had been defeated but not permanently destroyed, his armor remained intact, Belcorra’s vault was only partly examined, and Queen Quara Orshendiel still expected the company to kill the urdefhan warlord Khurfel. August Shepard nevertheless proposed beginning the day with a fortifying brew he and Talia Nightclaw had learned to prepare in Absalom, intended to strengthen those who drank it against the exertions ahead.

The preparation required several hours. Talia’s part appears to have been performed correctly; August’s was not, a distinction he insisted upon afterward with considerably greater enthusiasm than anyone else required. Something went wrong badly enough that the completed mixture produced nearly the opposite of its intended effect, and everyone who drank it became violently ill.

For approximately twelve hours the Roseguard remained incapable of returning safely to the Hunting Grounds. Talia devoted herself chiefly to keeping the others hydrated, while August cleaned up after himself, apologized when anyone permitted him to, and spent much of the day in a corner confronting the particular misery of having poisoned his companions through an attempt to protect them. Moadenn, whose recovery from reincarnation was progressing nearby, appears to have found the entire episode substantially funnier than August did.

Theron had warned beforehand that drinking first thing in the morning was unwise, and the day’s events vindicated him in the narrowest possible sense. This does not make the observation medically useful, but it does prevent me from calling it incorrect.

August’s later account of the failure deserves more attention than the ruined brew itself because it explains much of what followed. He did not regard the mistake merely as an unpleasant delay; by keeping the Roseguard in Yldaris when they had intended to be elsewhere, he believed he had made himself responsible for whatever danger found them there. “This is a level of failure that—well, I’ll figure out a way to live with it,” he said afterward. “I just don’t know how to live with it right now.”

Belcorra Haruvex appeared through the floor of their quarters late that afternoon. Her purpose was not difficult to infer, since the Roseguard had entered her private vault, destroyed the clay guardian made in her likeness, and defeated Caliddo, whom she identified as her cousin. She had evidently learned enough of those events to come looking for the people responsible, and she found them still ill, without their usual preparations for battle and in the middle of a settlement whose inhabitants had offered them refuge.

Kiri Steelpaws was nearest when Belcorra appeared. She vomited in the ghost’s direction, retrieved her axe, and attacked her, in that order. The first action is included because the surviving accounts agree upon it and because removing it would make the record less accurate merely to make the event more dignified.

The fight that followed was badly disordered. Weapons had to be drawn or retrieved while the company was already under attack, the failed brew continued to interfere with nearly everything they attempted, and several members were obliged to stop fighting long enough to be sick before resuming. Talia reached the doorway, vomited there, and tried to find a line for her crossbow through a room already crowded with companions, while Comet continued playing despite circumstances particularly hostile to wind instruments.

August attempted to protect and restore the others while visibly convinced that their predicament was his doing. That conclusion was only partly correct: his mistake explains why the Roseguard was still in Yldaris when Belcorra arrived, but there is no evidence that it caused her arrival. Belcorra came because her vault had been breached and Caliddo defeated, and she already knew of Yldaris, whose inhabitants had once been numbered among the forces beneath Gauntlight that she considered obedient.

The Roseguard had altered that allegiance without Belcorra’s knowledge, and the failed brew merely ensured that they were present when she discovered the alteration. The distinction between occasion, cause, and responsibility is useful to a historian and was of no apparent comfort to August.

Despite their condition, the Roseguard injured Belcorra severely enough that she abandoned the first room and passed through a wall toward Moadenn and the drow nearby. The company followed by more conventional means, carrying the fight directly among the people sheltering them. That movement later mattered more to Queen Quara than any attempt to divide responsibility for how the confrontation had begun.

Belcorra attempted a particularly destructive magical working once the pursuit reached the adjoining chamber. Theron, by then armed with the silver sword he had obtained in Absalom for use against incorporeal opponents, struck her while the spell was being formed and broke it before completion. The surviving accounts are unusually consistent that his intervention prevented much greater harm to everyone in the room.

The Roseguard destroyed Belcorra’s manifested form shortly afterward, marking their first direct victory over her. The terminology nevertheless requires qualification, because destruction of a ghost’s present manifestation is not equivalent to permanent destruction of the ghost. Belcorra could return within days unless whatever sustained her undeath was resolved, and the company already knew enough about such spirits to understand the difference.

The brew failed before Belcorra arrived; dignity failed during the fight.

Her unfinished purpose supplied one theoretical solution. Belcorra remained bound to the world by her intended destruction of Absalom, so allowing her to accomplish that purpose might conceivably have ended the haunting. No member of the Roseguard considered the prospective destruction of the city a useful treatment for the problem.

Queen Quara’s judgment arrived much sooner than Belcorra’s return. Whatever responsibility the Roseguard might assign among themselves, their presence had now brought a direct attack by Belcorra into Yldaris, and Quara was unwilling to expose her people to another. She refused August an extended apology and told them, “You need to leave this place. I have asked you for one thing, and you have brought doom on my house.”

Theron accepted the decision without argument, while Talia asked whether some channel of communication could remain open because the Roseguard still intended to kill Khurfel and fulfill the bargain previously made with Yldaris. Moadenn was permitted to stay and continue recovering, separating her circumstances from theirs. The Roseguard themselves understood that the settlement was no longer available as a refuge.

August lingered briefly at the closed entrance after the others departed. His faith in Alseta placed unusual significance upon thresholds, hospitality, and the relationships formed when one person admits another into a protected place, and being expelled from such a place after endangering its inhabitants struck precisely where the day’s earlier failure had already left him vulnerable. He expressed the hope that the doors might someday open to him again, then followed the others back into the Hunting Grounds.

The nearest defensible shelter readily available to them was Belcorra’s own vault. What had been an objective of investigation the previous day consequently became their temporary lodging, a use for which its original owner can safely be assumed not to have designed it.

Before settling there, the Roseguard revisited the proposed disposal of Caliddo’s armor. Their plan had been to use the peculiar Bag of Weasels to transform the plate permanently into an animal, reasoning that armor which no longer existed as armor could not reconstruct the graveknight around itself. A closer examination of the bag’s enchantment revealed the immediate defect: its transformative curse did not supersede curses already bound into an object, and graveknight armor is, among other things, emphatically cursed.

Caliddo’s armor therefore remained armor, and nothing had yet been withdrawn from the bag. The Roseguard at least discovered the flaw before proceeding with the repeated transformations they had proposed the previous evening. I should add, because later events require it, that the failure of this plan was not the full extent of the difficulty created by putting the armor there; the remaining consequences belong later in this account.

The unexplored chambers of the vault provided better results. One held substantial quantities of silver, gold, and platinum, while another contained shattered shelving, glass, and pottery consistent with some older explosion. A further room held weapons and armor of useful quality, including a warhammer fashioned from adamantine, but the most consequential discovery was neither monetary nor martial.

Beyond another locked door stood a dusty framework of copper and silver surrounded by shelves of preserved books. The device resembled the internal support of a lighthouse lantern, except that four places intended to hold lenses were empty. The books were research journals written by Belcorra Haruvex herself, and the diagrams within them identified the mechanism as the Fulcrum Lattice.

Talia began examining the journals that evening and continued through the night. They corrected the Roseguard’s understanding of the Fulcrum Lenses almost immediately, establishing that there were four rather than three: Crimson, Ochre, Emerald, and Ebon. Each contained a fragment of Nhimbaloth’s essence, and contact with a lens could impress some portion of that essence upon a living or undead bearer.

Belcorra had not failed to understand the danger involved. Her own notes show unease about bringing the four lenses together, which is significant because caution of this sort is not otherwise prominent in her surviving writings. She retained the Ebon Lens at the base of Gauntlight, gave the Emerald to the urdefhans, entrusted the Ochre to the drow, and concealed the Crimson among her undead gnome servants.

This distribution was a security measure founded upon politics rather than architecture. Belcorra did not expect the factions beneath Gauntlight to cooperate, and for centuries they did not. By keeping the lenses in the hands of groups that distrusted one another, she kept their combined danger inaccessible without needing to place all four behind a single lock.

The Lattice had nevertheless been built to gather them. Its construction allowed the four lenses to be mounted and directed together, overcoming the practical difficulty of carrying and manipulating them separately. More importantly, Belcorra’s journals explained what would happen if all four fragments of Nhimbaloth’s essence were concentrated within a single creature.

Her description of the result was total annihilation. The four fragments together would draw Nhimbaloth’s full gaze across the intervening distance and destroy the being upon whom that attention settled. Belcorra considered a single fragment an acceptable risk and the four together intolerable, which explains both her willingness to use the Ebon Lens and her insistence upon dispersing the others.

The machine had four empty places and one intended victim.

The discovery supplied the Roseguard with the first plausible method they had found for ending Belcorra permanently. They already possessed the Crimson and Ochre Lenses, while the Emerald remained in urdefhan hands and the Ebon was associated with Belcorra herself at the base of Gauntlight. If all four essences could be forced upon her, Nhimbaloth’s own power might accomplish what repeated destruction of Belcorra’s ghostly form could not.

August disliked the proposal for reasons that require no elaborate theology. He had spent much of the campaign opposing the destruction and corruption of souls, and deliberately arranging for an Outer God associated with obliteration to direct its attention upon one was not a method he found easy to distinguish morally merely because the intended victim was Belcorra Haruvex. He nevertheless recognized that the alternatives they knew were considerably worse.

Theron described the plan as possessing “a certain poetic justice,” since Belcorra might be destroyed by the very power she had intended to turn against others. August agreed with the description while remaining uneasy about the act itself. Their disagreement was therefore not about what they intended to do, but about whether necessity made the means any less distasteful.

The Roseguard remained in the vault overnight while Talia continued through the journals. They repaired what equipment they could, redistributed several recovered objects, and waited until the lingering effects of the morning’s brew finally passed. With Yldaris unavailable to them, Belcorra’s former storehouse had become their base by default.

On the morning of 10 Kuthona, August and Talia noticed a concealed door in Caliddo’s former chamber that had escaped attention during the preceding fighting and exhaustion. Beyond it was a curved room covered from floor to ceiling in spiraling channels filled with silver, an arrangement recognizable from other teleportation chambers within Gauntlight. Restoring it would give the company another route between the lower Vaults and the levels above, a considerably more attractive prospect now that they could no longer pass freely through Yldaris.

The Roseguard attempted the restoration that morning. Talia’s part in the working is preserved clearly enough, but the surviving record no longer retains a reliable identity for the person who directed it, while preserving the result with considerably greater certainty. The teleportation chamber did not resume functioning, and the failed working instead brought something else into the room.

The creature possessed an exposed and disproportionately large brain, long arms, and a compact lower body. Its mere presence produced a kind of psychic interference that made coherent thought more difficult, and Talia addressed it while the Roseguard reached for weapons. The answer came without passing through the air between them, heard by everyone present directly within the mind: “Who summoned me?”

The chamber still moved travelers; direction had become negotiable.
